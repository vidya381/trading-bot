## Step 59: `repairPosition` — the repair tool that recovered two stuck live bots, designed in a separate session before a line was written, and verified end to end on the exchange by the operator

Date: 2026-08-21 · **NOT COMMITTED** as of writing. Six files sit modified on
disk for this fix, alongside entries 57 and 58's; the operator commits separately
after this entry.

### What this step is, and what it is NOT

This is **fix 3** of the chain that began with two stuck live testnet bots.
Entry **57** stopped the halt's cancel sweep destroying a fill count it had not
recorded; entry **58** stopped `resume()` re-entering `running` on books already
flagged untrustworthy. Neither could help the two bots already damaged. **This
one did**, and Part 4 is the operator's live proof of it.

**It is a correction tool for already-damaged state, not a fix to the underlying
bug.** The defect that produced this damage — a DCA position that never
decrements on exit-side fills, because `applyEntry` has no inverse — is **fix 5,
and it is not built**. A future bot can develop exactly this drift again, and
this tool would have to be run against it again. Nothing in this entry should be
read as the underlying bug being closed. **Fix 4** (reconciliation's
zero-tolerance timing against the 30-second poll) is also not built.

**The split held for the author's half:** no `wrangler` command, no deploy, no
HTTP call, no query against real D1, no browser. Every figure in Part 3 is from
running the suite on this machine. **Part 4 is entirely the operator's** — every
live result there was produced and reported by them, not observed here.

## PART 1 — DESIGNED BEFORE BUILT, AND WHY THAT WAS WORTH A WHOLE SESSION

This fix got a **design-only session before any code**, deliberately, because it
is the highest-risk change in the chain: **it rewrites a live bot's financial
state.** Everything else in this system derives numbers forward and refuses to
correct them — section 9 halts and alerts and never auto-corrects, `checkOpenOrders`
reports what it folded and what it refused, `applyMissedFills` refuses to
synthesise a fill id. This is the first thing here that OVERWRITES a position.
Designing it in the same breath as writing it would have meant discovering the
open questions with a half-built implementation already shaping the answers.

### The brief's premise was corrected by that design session

The chain's working belief, carried from the first investigation, was that the
two bots had **lost fill data** — that executions had happened on the exchange
and were permanently unattributable because the orders went terminal.

Reading the actual numbers said otherwise. On both bots `orders.filled_quantity`
and `SUM(trades.quantity)` **agree exactly** — the local fill records are whole
and internally consistent. The reconciliation halt text captured earlier had the
bot believing `0.00038534`; it now holds `0.00115434`. Between the halt and now,
the poll evidently **did** catch up and apply the missing executions under their
real ids.

**So the live damage was fix 5's, not lost data:** the exit sell's fills were
recorded against the ORDER and never subtracted from the POSITION.

That mattered practically, and it made the build smaller: the repair is a
**subtraction over records the bot already holds**, not a reconstruction of
missing ones. It does not need to invent, attribute, or synthesise anything.

### The one decision escalated to the operator rather than taken silently

**The cost model for a partially exited position** — because the model has no
defined meaning for one. Within a cycle `quantity` and `cost` only ever grow
together, and `#completeCycle` zeroes both. A partial exit is a state
`DcaPosition` was never asked to represent, so the repair had to *choose* a
semantics rather than derive one.

Two candidates:

| | **Proportional** (`cost × remainingFraction`) | **Unchanged** (`cost` = full buy notional) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `averageEntryPrice` | **preserved** — the coin still held WAS bought at that average | **inflated up to 3.7×** on bot-b23y63 |
| Stop-loss / take-profit | stay meaningful | both trigger prices move with the inflation |
| Capital headroom | restored in proportion to what was sold | unchanged |

The design session recommended **proportional**, on the grounds that
`averageEntryPrice` drives **both** risk thresholds ([dca.ts:461](../../src/strategies/dca.ts#L461),
[dca.ts:473](../../src/strategies/dca.ts#L473)) and the alternative would have
left the repaired bot with corrupt future risk behaviour — fixing the quantity
while breaking the prices it is judged against. It is also the continuous form of
a rule this system already applies discretely: a completed cycle zeroes `cost`
and restores the whole allocation.

**The operator chose proportional.** That decision is the reason this entry
exists as built.

## PART 2 — WHAT WAS BUILT

### `repairPosition(actor, { commit })`, report-by-default

`commit: false` is the default and is a **pure read**: it computes the entire
before/after diff, runs every gate, and returns which one would block — writing
nothing. That inversion is deliberate and matches the reserve-judgment pattern
this project already runs on everywhere else.

### What it reads

`#config()`, `#state()`, the **full** order history from Durable Object storage
(nothing ever deletes an order — `storage.delete` is used only for the alarm, so
this is the bot's complete lifetime), the `bot.cycle_completed` /
`bot.liquidation_filled` rows from `audit_log`, and **one fresh live
`getOrderStatus` per live-cycle order**, at `routine` priority so bookkeeping
cannot draw on section 5.4's risk-exit reserve.

**The live call is not optional, and this is the load-bearing point of the whole
design.** The repair's output is a subtraction over local records, and local
records are *precisely what this chain of bugs corrupts*. A repair that trusted
them could only ever reproduce the drift it was built to remove — while looking
authoritative, and writing a confidently wrong number over a visibly wrong one.
That is worse than the status quo. So every live-cycle order is read back and
must agree exactly.

### Ten gates, in order, refuse-whole — never partial

Fix 57's shape applied to a much larger write. Every check runs before the single
`#mutateState`, and the first failure returns a report naming the gate and every
reason behind it.

1. **strategy** — DCA only; grid is explicitly excluded (its position is the
   ladder, a different reconstruction with different arithmetic)
2. **status** — halted only, so the books are not rewritten under a live pipeline
3. **unsettled orders** — nothing in the live cycle may still be moving
4. **unreadable orders** — section 5.6: an unreachable exchange is not data
5. **local/remote fill disagreement** — exact equality, per order
6. **partition sanity** — the cycle boundary must be derivable and consistent
   with `cycleCount`
7. **cycle audit cross-check** — an independent second source (below)
8. **negative quantity** — a bot cannot hold less than nothing
9. **cost exceeds allocation** — a repaired cost above the allocation would leave
   `#placeBuy`'s own budget permanently negative
10. **exit order** — `exitOrderId` may be cleared only when it is terminal **and**
    its fills are inside the sum this repair just subtracted

### The cycle partition, and why it is corroborated rather than trusted

bot-bl4e7c has one closed cycle and one live one, so "sum everything" would be
wrong. The boundary is **a fully-filled sell order**, and that marker is sound
rather than heuristic: `position: EMPTY_POSITION` is written at runtime by
exactly `#completeCycle` and `#completeLiquidation`, both reached only from
`#applyFillToOrder`'s `isExit && effect.fullyFilled` — which is exactly the
transition to `filled`. And `filled` cannot be asserted onto an order from
outside: `closeOrder` accepts only cancelled/rejected/expired, and
`#foldTerminalState` states that "an order is recorded as filled by applying its
fills, never by asserting the end state over them" — a rule entry 57 extended to
the second door. Ordering comes from the numeric sequence inside the
`clientOrderId`; `nextSequence` is set to 0 at creation and only ever
incremented, never reset by `#completeCycle`, so it is a total order **across**
cycles, which the exchange-supplied `createdAt` is not.

**That derivation is then checked against a second, independent source as a HARD
GATE, not derived once and trusted.** `#completeCycle` audits `entries` — that
cycle's buy-fill count — and `#completeLiquidation` audits its own row; each
fully-filled sell produced exactly one of the two, in time order. Gate 7 aligns
the partition against those rows and refuses on any disagreement.

### The cost model and the sold leg's PnL

`cost` is scaled by the fraction still held, in **one** rounding
(`divideRounded(boughtCost × quantity, bought, "half-even")`) rather than by a
pre-divided fraction, which would round the fraction and then round the product.
`averageEntryPrice` is recomputed with the same formula `applyEntry` uses.

**The sold leg's realized PnL is booked into `realizedGross` in the same write**,
because scaling `cost` down without it would simply lose that leg — `realizedGross`
is written only by the two cycle-completion paths, and neither ran.

It is computed from **actual fill notionals** — `Σ mul(fill.price, fill.quantity)`
— rather than from `filledQuantity × order.price`, which is what `#completeCycle`
uses. **This is a deliberate, operator-approved improvement on that path, not an
inconsistency to be avoided:** the per-fill detail is right there in
`order.fills`, and a limit price is only an approximation of what the fills
actually executed at.

### `additionalBuysUsed` is preserved, never recomputed

It counts entries taken while `position.quantity > ZERO` — a **runtime
observation at the moment each fill was applied**, not a property of any order,
and therefore not recoverable from order history after the fact. Replaying it
would be faithful only if every buy fill is present, which is exactly what a
repair cannot assume. Preserving the stored value keeps the bot's remaining buy
budget as it was; recomputing it could silently hand back budget already spent.

### A race guard, added during the build and NOT numbered as an eleventh gate

Self-identified while writing, beyond the approved design. This operation makes
**multiple live exchange calls**, and every one of them suspends the object —
step 21 measured that an RPC or an alarm is delivered into exactly that window.
`#mutateState` prevents two writes reverting each other; it cannot protect a
**decision** computed against state that has since moved, and this entire
computation is one such decision. So the mutate re-reads and refuses to apply if
`position`, `realizedGross`, `exitOrderId` or `status` changed underneath the
reads, reporting `blockedBy: "concurrent state change during the exchange reads"`.

**Deliberately unnumbered**, so "ten gates" stays an accurate description of the
design rather than quietly becoming eleven.

### A separate finding: the completed-cycle dust leak — DETECTED, REPORTED, NOT FIXED

`#placeTakeProfitSell` sizes the exit from `position.quantity` through
`validateOrder`, whose quantity **"rounds DOWN unconditionally, on either side"**,
and `#completeCycle` then zeroes the position regardless. So a completed cycle can
leave real base behind that the bot no longer models, once per cycle, forever.

**This is independent of the bug being repaired and was deliberately NOT folded
into the repair's arithmetic.** Absorbing it would invent base the live cycle
never bought — making one number wrong in a new way while fixing another. It is
emitted in the report's `findings` so it is visible, and left alone. It also
**cannot** shift gate 7, because gate 7 compares fill *counts* and the leak is a
*quantity* residue.

### Route

`POST /api/bots/:id/repair-position`, human-actor only, report by default with
`?commit=true` the only thing that writes. **Reachable while entry 58's drift
alert stands** — which is the whole point, since resolving that alert is what
this exists to enable. It never changes status, and a halted bot stays halted.

## PART 4 — LIVE VERIFICATION (the operator's, not the author's)

**Every result in this part was produced and reported by the operator.** No live
call was made from the authoring session, and none of this is the author's
observation.

### 1. A process finding, caught before it misled anyone: nothing had been deployed

The operator discovered that **no deploy had occurred since this bug chain
began**. All three fixes existed only in the working tree; the live testnet
server was still running pre-fix code. They ran the project's real deploy
(`npm run deploy:testnet`) before anything could be tested live.

**This is worth recording as a process finding in its own right.** The failure
mode it nearly produced is specific and nasty: hitting the new endpoint against
an undeployed Worker returns a route-not-found, which looks exactly like a
routing bug in code that is in fact correct — and the wrong conclusion would have
been drawn about working code. It was caught before that happened. (The chain's
working agreement has a norm about verifying against what is actually deployed; I
could not locate a document in this repo that numbers it, so it is described here
rather than cited by number.)

### 2. Report mode against both live bots, post-deploy

Both returned `outcome: "would_repair"`, with **`agrees: true` on every order's
local-versus-remote fill comparison**.

**This settled the one genuinely unknown fact in the whole design**, live and
affirmatively: **Gemini's sandbox still answers `/v1/order/status` for orders
cancelled days earlier.** That was flagged at the start of the build session as
unknowable from source, with gate 4 built to fail closed if the answer turned out
to be no. The answer is yes, and gate 4 never had to fire.

### 3. Three independent derivations, in agreement, to the last digit

The report-mode numbers matched — **exactly** — figures derived three separate
times, twice before any code existed:

| Bot | Before | After | Derived by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bot-b23y63` | `0.00158519` | **`0.00043085`** | operator's manual arithmetic · design session's projection · live report mode |
| `bot-bl4e7c` | `0.00126687` | **`0.00092962`** | the same three, independently |

Three independent routes to the same number, the last of them against real
exchange data, is about as strong as pre-commit evidence gets in this project.

### 4. Commit

The operator ran `commit: true` against both. Both returned `outcome:
"repaired"`, `committed: true`, with **`exitOrderId` and `exitKind` cleared to
`null`** on both.

### 5. The end-to-end proof, and a composition result

The operator then placed a **real Liquidate order on `bot-b23y63` against the
repaired quantity. It filled: 0.00043085 BTC at 68169.88** — a real trade with a
real timestamp — and the position is now genuinely zero.

**And step 56's archive gate correctly recognised the bot as flat and made
Archive available again.**

That second half is the part worth stating separately: it is proof that **fix 3
and step 56's gate compose correctly**, not merely that each works alone. The
archive gate reads the position it is given; had the repair written a number that
was wrong in a new way, the gate would have read the wrong number and either
blocked a flat bot or released a non-flat one. It did neither. A repaired
quantity was sold on a real exchange for exactly that quantity, and a second,
independently-built gate then agreed the bot was flat.

**`bot-bl4e7c`'s liquidation order was placed and is PENDING FILL as of this
entry.** Not filled, not confirmed. Nothing here claims otherwise.

## PART 5 — NOT DONE / NOT VERIFIED

- **Fix 5 — the forward-looking position-decrement fix in `dca.ts` — is NOT
  built.** `applyEntry` still has no inverse, and `#applyFillToOrder` still leaves
  `position` untouched on an exit-side partial fill. **This repair is a one-time
  correction of already-damaged state, not a structural fix.** A future bot can
  develop the same drift, and this tool would have to be run against it again.
  Nothing here closes the underlying defect and no timeline is committed to.
- **Fix 4 — reconciliation's zero-tolerance timing against the 30-second poll**,
  and the related Gemini `updatedAt = createdAt` gap: not built, not started.
- **The completed-cycle dust leak (Part 2): detected and reported only.** Real,
  independent of this bug, deliberately not folded into the repair's arithmetic,
  and unfixed.
- **`bot-bl4e7c`'s liquidation was PENDING at the time of writing.** Its repair
  committed and its order was placed; the fill is not confirmed here.
- **Grid-strategy repair is explicitly out of scope.** Gate 1 refuses it, with a
  test asserting the refusal rather than leaving it to chance.
- **The race guard is deliberately not one of the ten gates**, so the design's
  "ten gates" description stays accurate.
- **Nothing in this entry is committed as of writing.** Six files modified on
  disk for this fix, alongside entries 57 and 58's.
