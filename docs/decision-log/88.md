## PART 1 — THE STAGE 3 CONTRACT, BUILT AGAINST SPECIFIC WRONG ANSWERS

`TRAILING_STOP_DERIVE_FIELDS` is `["trailPct"]` — one field, the whole set (22.2
decision 1). `DERIVE_MODEL_SETTINGS` gains its entry through the same
`settingsFor` as grid and DCA: same temperature, same seed, same JSON-schema
construction. No special casing, per 22.4 touchpoint 4's warning that **"a
one-field parameter set does not make a proposal easier to justify — the one
field carries all of the risk that grid spreads across seven."**

**`trailingStopFieldContract()` is not a description of the parameter.** It is
written to head off two specific answers a competent model would plausibly give,
each of which looks entirely correct on the page:

**1. `trailPct` does double duty, and there is no separate stop-loss field.** It
is the trail distance below the high-water mark *and* the initial stop distance
from the entry price before any new high is made. A model reasoning from a
generic trailing-stop definition would treat it as a trail distance alone — and
would then either invent a `stopLossPct` (rejected by `requireExactFields`, so at
least loud), or, far worse, **pick a trail value on the assumption that some
separate stop-loss is protecting the position in the meantime.** There is none.
That answer parses, validates, and is wrong in the one direction that matters:
the operator approves a bot they believe is protected twice and is protected
once. The contract states both jobs explicitly, as lettered clauses (a) and (b).

**2. There is no profit-activation threshold.** The trail is live from the first
price the bot sees, not from some level of profit (22.2 decision 2, 22.6). "It
starts trailing once I am up X%" is a real and common variant, and a model
assuming it is describing a different strategy while producing a valid-looking
number for this one.

The contract also names what the strategy **does not have** — no order size (the
single entry is sized by `allocatedCapital`), no take-profit (the trail *is* the
exit), no averaging down — because `requireExactFields` refuses an invented
field, and a model told what is absent is less likely to invent it than one left
to infer absence from a short list.

**⚠ THE BOUNDS ARE IMPORTED, NOT RESTATED.** `TRAIL_PCT_MIN` / `TRAIL_PCT_MAX`
come from `strategies/trailing-stop.ts` — the module that *enforces* them — and
are interpolated into the prompt text. A second copy here could disagree with the
validator, and the failure would be perfectly quiet in the worst way: the model
would be told one range, answer inside it, and be refused against another. The
refusal would read to a human as the model disobeying an instruction it had
actually been given correctly. The prompt also carries the bounds' own honesty —
that they are **provisional** (22.5 #1), a starting range and not a backtested
result — in the same words `validateTrailingStopParams` refuses with.

Entry 87's tripwire (`DERIVE_MODEL_SETTINGS` has no `trailing_stop` key) **fired
on the first run of this work**, exactly as designed, and named PIN ONE as the
assertion to write. It has been converted into that assertion:
`TRAILING_STOP_PROPOSAL_FIELDS` is now pinned equal to
`TRAILING_STOP_DERIVE_FIELDS`. An absence-assertion that is no longer true is not
a guard.

## PART 2 — ⚠ EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY (22.5 #4) REMAINS OPEN. THIS WORK DID NOT RESOLVE IT

**Stated first so it cannot be read past: nothing here settles 22.5 open question
4.** It asks whether Stage 2's trend-detection heuristic is supported by the
evidence Stage 1 currently gathers, or whether new evidence fields are needed. It
is still open, and this entry is not a decision on it.

**The FIELDS can express the judgment.** `candles.change_pct` (net move, first
open to last close) read against `candles.range_pct` (total travel, high to low)
is a directional-efficiency measure — a large change relative to range is a
trending market, a small change across a large range is an oscillating one, which
is precisely the grid-versus-trailing-stop distinction. Beneath them sit up to 24
ordered OHLCV buckets with timestamps: a real series, adequate for telling a
progressive move from a round trip. The task section has always asked the model
about **"recent volatility and trend character"**; the vocabulary is not new.

**Three limits make that weak in practice, and none is a prompt problem:**

1. **The window is ~1 day of 1-minute bars, and 21.7 #1 is still open.** Gemini's
   `/v2/candles` takes no time-range parameter and returns a fixed recent window;
   `since` filters locally and cannot reach past it. A trend judged over 24 hours
   of minute bars is **intraday noise**, not the sustained directional move this
   strategy needs to be the right choice. 22.5 #4 names this constraint itself:
   *"a trend judgement made over two hours of candles is a different claim from
   one made over two weeks."*
2. **Testnet's market is currently fiction ([86.md](86.md)).** Gemini's sandbox
   BTCUSD showed **1439 of 1440 daily candles identical at 78172.34, zero volume,
   and a crossed book**. There, `change_pct` and `range_pct` are both ~0 and **no
   trend judgment is possible at all** — not a wrong one, an impossible one.
3. **There is no news or sentiment evidence.** `news.status` is `NOT COLLECTED`
   (decision log 30, vendor paused), so there is no catalyst information behind a
   directional bet. The evidence item says so explicitly and warns it must not be
   read as a quiet market.

**⚠ NO DERIVED "DIRECTIONAL EFFICIENCY" FIELD WAS ADDED, AND THE OMISSION IS THE
DECISION.** Computing `change_pct / range_pct` and emitting it as its own
evidence id was considered and rejected. It would compute cleanly, cite honestly,
and read as a stronger signal than the data underneath it supports — **dressing
up a shallow window as a trend measurement**, which is the failure mode
[86.md](86.md) was written about in a different layer. The underlying limit is
the window, and no arithmetic over it fixes that. 22.5 #4 treats new evidence
fields as an explicit open decision requiring a recorded choice; adding one
quietly while building a prompt contract is exactly the "silent default" 22.5's
preamble forbids.

## PART 3 — ⚠ SPEC 22.7 BLOCKED A PREMATURE LAUNCH, AND THE INSTRUCTION WAS NOT FOLLOWED AS GIVEN

**The instruction going into this session was to enable the strategy fully:**
pin `ASSESS_STRATEGIES` to include `trailing_stop`, with — quoted — *"no special
caution flag or newly-added marker needed in the prompt text — this launches with
the same standing as grid/DCA, no different treatment."*

**That directly contradicts spec 22.7**, which is explicit and bolded:

> **This strategy must run on testnet, in bots created manually by a human — not
> suggested by the pipeline — for a real observation period before Stage 2 is
> permitted to recommend it in any proposal.**
>
> **Minimum observation period: [...] not less than one full week of live testnet
> operation across at least a few bots.**
>
> **Do not let LLM-suggested trailing-stop bots go live before this observation
> period, even if implementation and tests are complete.**

Adding `trailing_stop` to `ASSESS_STRATEGIES` **is** permitting Stage 2 to
recommend it. And **the current operating history is zero**: `bot-ts1` is the
only trailing-stop bot ever run, and it never filled an entry — it halted with
`entry_unfilled` against its own retry cap ([86.md](86.md), [87.md](87.md)). The
floor has not been approached, let alone met. That is not a judgement call about
duration, which 22.7 does leave to the operator; it is the floor beneath it.

**So the instruction was not followed as originally given.** The question was put
to the operator with the gate quoted, and the answer was to hold Stage 2 closed
and implement the gate as a checked, documented subset. This entry records that
the change asked for was not made, and why.

22.7's reasoning is empirical and specific to this codebase, which is why it
outweighed "implementation and tests are complete": decision log 81's PriceFeed
leak surfaced as a **billing anomaly a month into production**; decision log 82's
incomplete halt was found because a **real bot left a sell order uncancelled**,
with 3,281 tests passing at the time. This strategy's failure mode is **silently
not exiting** (22.3) — the worst kind to discover through a proposal a human
approved because the page looked complete.

### What was built instead

**`ASSESS_STRATEGIES` is no longer a literal.** It was
`["dca", "grid"] as const` under a comment reading *"There is no third"* — and a
third arrived (spec 22) while the literal did not move, which is how a strategy
came to exist that this file did not know about. It is now:

```
STRATEGY_KEYS: Readonly<Record<StrategyType, true>>   ← completeness by COMPILER
  → ALL_ASSESS_STRATEGIES                             ← derived, never hand-listed
  → assessStrategiesExcluding(WITHHELD_FROM_ASSESS)   ← the gate, as a pure function
  → ASSESS_STRATEGIES
```

A `Record<StrategyType, true>` is a compile error if a strategy is missing and an
excess-property error if one is invented, so **no test assertion is load-bearing
for the union's completeness**. This file takes the direct `StrategyType` import
that `proposal-shape.ts` and `staleness.ts` cannot — verified rather than
assumed: dashboard **app** code imports only four `src/` modules (`proposal-shape`,
`staleness`, `shared/alert-types`, `shared/money`), this file is imported only by
dashboard **test** files, and `dashboard/tsconfig.app.json:39` excludes those from
`tsc -b`. `derive-prompt.ts` next door has imported `StrategyType` on exactly
these terms all along.

**`WITHHELD_FROM_ASSESS` names `trailing_stop`** with 22.7 quoted in full and the
concrete reason stated — zero completed operating history, `bot-ts1` never filled
— rather than a bare flag a later reader would have to research.

**The prompt text is derived from the same set, not hand-copied**: the count word,
the strategy list, the task sentence and the response contract all render from
`ASSESS_STRATEGIES`, so the prompt cannot drift from the gate. A withheld
strategy is absent from the prompt entirely — describing a choice the parser will
refuse is how a model is set up to fail a rule it was implicitly offered. Output
is **byte-identical** for two strategies; the long-standing prompt-text pins pass
unchanged.

**`STRATEGY_DESCRIPTIONS` is a complete `Record<StrategyType, string>`**, so a
strategy cannot become assessable without a real description existing. Trailing
stop's is already written to 22.4 touchpoint 3's standard — it states the market
condition the strategy needs (a sustained move in one direction) and what it costs
(giving back the configured percentage from the peak, with no second entry to
average down if the move reverses early). The tests assert those phrases, because
a placeholder would satisfy a length check and not a content one.

Seven new tests pin: the union is complete at runtime as well as in the type;
**every strategy is classified as assessable or withheld, never neither** — so a
fourth strategy cannot default into "yes"; `trailing_stop` appears nowhere in the
rendered prompt; and the lift works.

### ⚠ THE "ONE LINE" CLAIM WAS OVERSTATED, AND THE CORRECTION IS RECORDED

The design goal was that clearing 22.7 be one deletion rather than a
rediscovery. **Verified by simulating it** — deleting `"trailing_stop"` from
`WITHHELD_FROM_ASSESS` and running everything:

* **`tsc` stays clean.** Nothing downstream needs discovering: no schema branch,
  no parser arm, no missing description. `assess-parse.ts` and
  `assess-resubmit.ts` both gate on `ASSESS_STRATEGIES`, so Stage 3 opens with
  Stage 2 automatically.
* **Four assertions fail.** Three are the gate tests that name the withheld
  state; the fourth is the long-standing pin asserting the prompt says *"exactly
  one of the two lowercase strings"*, which correctly becomes three.

So **"one line" is true of the source and false of the suite**, and the docblocks
were rewritten to say exactly that rather than keep a tidier claim. The four
failures are **the gate announcing itself**: each names the withheld state it was
written to describe, and updating them is the deliberate act of recording that
22.7 was satisfied. That is the correct behaviour for a checked gate, not
breakage — but it is not what "one line" implies, and the difference is the sort
that erodes trust in a docblock.

## PART 4 — THE STALENESS CORRECTION (22.5 #2)

**The 60-minute threshold recorded in [87.md](87.md) was wrong, by the exact
reasoning 22.5 open question 2 rules out by name.**

It was set by applying `staleness.ts`'s own relative-versus-absolute-parameter
test: `trailPct` is a percentage applied to a runtime high-water mark, so it
names no absolute price; the single entry is sized by `allocatedCapital`, an
absolute quote amount checked against the reference price, exactly as DCA's
`baseOrderSize` is. Same structure, therefore DCA's number.

That is a coherent argument answering the wrong question, and 22.5 #2 had
anticipated it verbatim: *"What it must not be is a default fallback or **a copy
of DCA's number chosen because DCA is the closest structural analogue** — the
structural analogy does not hold for the property the threshold measures."* The
number written was that copy.

**What the threshold actually measures** is how promptly a price drop is noticed,
because that is what this strategy's whole risk control rests on — not how a
parameter is denominated. 22.5 #2's own lean: *"at or tighter than grid's — not
looser."*

**Corrected to 15 minutes, matching grid**, by the operator's decision. It
remains **policy measured against nothing**, in the same category as the two
beside it, and must not acquire authority by being the corrected one.

**Recorded forward rather than silently overwritten**, which is the part worth
keeping: [87.md](87.md) gained a correction block at its head and its README row
gained a ⚠ annotation, both naming the superseded number and why it was wrong.
`DEFAULT_STALENESS_POLICY`'s docblock and `staleness.test.ts`'s assertion carry
the same reasoning, so a reader arriving at any of the four sees the correction
rather than only the answer. The distinctness assertions re-pointed correctly:
grid and trailing stop now share a value and DCA is the outlier, with the reason
recorded so the missing assertion does not read as an omission.

### ⚠ THE STANDING OPEN ITEM, STATED PLAINLY

**22.5 #4 — evidence sufficiency for a trend judgment — is the real remaining
blocker, and it sits *before* 22.7's observation period rather than after it.**

22.7 requires a week of live testnet operation across a few bots before Stage 2
may recommend this strategy. But the first live trailing-stop assessment on
testnet would currently be **reasoning about a dead market**: Gemini's sandbox
BTCUSD is the frozen, zero-volume, crossed-book series [86.md](86.md) documents,
where the two fields a trend judgment rests on are both ~0. Running the
observation period on that data would produce a week of operating history that
says nothing about whether the strategy or its Stage 2 heuristic works.

So the order is: settle where honest trend evidence comes from (a deeper window
per 21.7 #1, a different venue or environment, or new evidence fields decided
under 22.5 #4) — **then** run 22.7's observation period — **then** delete the
line in `WITHHELD_FROM_ASSESS`. Doing the last of those first is what this
session's original instruction would have done.
