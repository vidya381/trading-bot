## PART 1 — THE FINDING: THE NUMBER WAS NEVER LIVE-SOURCED

**`0.00037572` did not come from tonight's investigation, from any live D1 query,
or from any live API response.** It is a **2026-08-05** figure — nineteen days
old at the moment entry 71 used it — and it lives in **two source-code
comments** describing a **different, already-fixed root cause**.

`git log -S"37572" --all` over the whole repository returns **exactly two
commits, and no third**:

| Commit | Date | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| `89044b8` | 2026-08-05 | `fix: grid slot collisions from same-instant fills` |
| `2b3fc29` | 2026-08-24 | `Close out bot-4xcq8p and bot-3trlgb: already self-resolved before repair was needed` |

The first introduced the number **into source comments**. The second is entry
**71** itself, committing the decision-log text. There is no live-data commit
between them, and no other appearance anywhere in the tree.

### The two comments, quoted verbatim

**`src/strategies/grid.ts:634`**, the `claimSlot` docblock — text unchanged since
`89044b8`:

> *"`withSlot` writes unconditionally, and on 2026-08-05 that silently lost two
> replacement sells on bot-4xcq8p. Four ladder buys filled in the same instant;
> the poll folds them one at a time, and folding the fill at level 0 placed its
> replacement sell into level 1 — the level still holding the buy that the very
> next iteration was about to fold. … **`0.00037572` BTC ended up held with no
> sell resting against it**, on a bot that went on reporting itself healthy."*

**`src/durable-objects/grid-bot-instance.test.ts:690`**, in the regression test's
header comment:

> *"THE 2026-08-05 REGRESSION, in its original shape. … On bot-4xcq8p that
> happened **twice in one pass** and stranded **`0.00037572`** BTC."*

### It describes a DIFFERENT bug from the one entries 70 and 71 addressed

The Aug-5 mechanism is a **same-instant slot-collision race**: `withSlot`
overwriting a level that still held a live resting order, so two replacement
sells were lost in a single fold pass. That was **fixed on 2026-08-05** by
`claimSlot`, in the same commit that wrote the comment.

Entries 70 and 71 addressed the **multi-execution replacement-sizing bug** —
`fullyFilled` being true on an order's *last* execution rather than its only one.
Entry 70 lists the `claimSlot` collision as **one of five known routes** to
uncovered inventory (line 87), but it is **not** the route entry 70 fixed, and
`0.00037572` is the Aug-5 collision's figure, not a measurement of the
multi-execution leak.

### How it entered the chain, stated exactly

**Entry 70 never mentions the number.** `grep -c "37572" docs/decision-log/70.md`
returns **`0`**. Its first and only appearance in the decision log is entry
**71**, at line 58, as the Stage D design's falsifiable prediction:

> *"The prediction was therefore specific and checkable: `heldQuantity` should
> read **`0.00037572`** on `bot-4xcq8p` and **`0.00018835`** on `bot-3trlgb`,
> matching the previously reported uncovered figures."*

**That is the whole of it.** A number that was true of `bot-4xcq8p` on **2026-08-05**,
under a bug fixed that same day, was carried into a 2026-08-24 investigation
**as if it were that bot's current uncovered quantity** — and it sat there
**unlabelled as historical, alongside genuinely live, operator-verified figures,
with nothing in the record distinguishing the two.**

## PART 2 — THE ARITHMETIC THAT CONFIRMS IT, RATHER THAN MERELY FITTING IT

The identification above is not a plausible story assembled after the fact. **It
is an exact decimal match against an independently sourced figure.**

Entry 71 records, `[OPERATOR, per entry 71]`, a real
`reconciliation_meaningful_uncovered_held_inventory` alert on `bot-4xcq8p`
reporting: **held `0.00023986`**, **covered `0.00018786`**, **uncovered
`0.00005200`**.

Three identities, all exact to the last recorded digit:

| Identity | Result | |
|---|---|---|
| `0.00018786 + 0.00005200` | `0.00023986` | the alert is internally consistent |
| **`0.00018786 × 2`** | **`0.00037572`** | **exact** |
| `0.00018786 − 0.00005200` | `0.00013586` | entry 71's unexplained difference |

**`0.00037572` is exactly two instances of `0.00018786`** — and `0.00018786` is
this bot's **per-rung buy quantity**, the amount a single resting sell covers.

That decomposition matches the Aug-5 comment's **own stated mechanism, word for
word**: *"lost **two** replacement sells"*, *"happened **twice** in one pass"*.
Two lost sells, one rung each. **Exactly, not approximately.**

### Why this is confirmation and not a fitted narrative

**The corroborating figure `0.00018786` appears nowhere in this codebase.**
`grep -rn "18786"` across the repository (excluding `node_modules`) returns **no
hits at all**. It exists only in the operator's live alert text, obtained during
tonight's investigation, entirely independently of the Aug-5 comment.

So the two sides of the identity have **separate, non-overlapping origins**: one
is a nineteen-day-old source comment, the other a live alert read tonight. **A
number invented, mistranscribed, or reasoned to could not land exactly on twice
an independently obtained rung size while also matching that comment's stated
"twice in one pass".** This is what distinguishes confirmation from a story that
merely fits.

For completeness, the alternatives that do **not** hold: **no** integer multiple
of the uncovered figure `0.00005200` reaches `0.00037572` (×7 = `0.00036400`,
×8 = `0.00041600`), and `0.00037572` matches **no single alert's reported
*uncovered* amount** — the one alert on record reports `0.00005200` uncovered.
**It is a sum of two episodes** — but of the **2026-08-05 collision pass**, not
of the August 12/14/17 alert series.

### The asymmetry that made the mixing possible

The two figures entry 71 grouped as *"the previously reported uncovered figures"*
have **different and unequal provenance**:

| Figure | Bot | Earliest appearance in this repository |
|---|---|---|
| `0.00037572` | `bot-4xcq8p` | **`89044b8`, 2026-08-05** — a source comment, nineteen days stale |
| `0.00018835` | `bot-3trlgb` | **`2b3fc29`, 2026-08-24** — today, entry 71 itself |

`git log -S"18835" --all` returns **only** today's commit. `0.00018835` has **no
source anywhere in this codebase predating tonight**; it is genuinely fresh.
`0.00037572` is not.

**This is the precise shape of the problem.** Two numbers, presented in one
sentence as a matched pair of "previously reported" figures, one live and one
nineteen days old — and the record carried nothing to tell them apart.

## PART 3 — CONFIRMED: NEITHER ENTRY 70 NOR ENTRY 71 REQUIRES REVISION

Each conclusion's dependency was **checked against its own text and its own
tests**, not assumed.

### Entry 70 — no revision warranted

**Entry 70 never cites `0.00037572` at all** (`grep -c` → `0`). Its Stages A/B/C
are justified **entirely by code paths**, each pinned by **local tests**:

- **Stage A** — `fullyFilled` being `filledQuantity === order.quantity`, true on
  an order's *last* execution rather than its only one, so a replacement sell was
  sized from one slice of a multi-execution fill.
- **Stage B** — the `claimSlot` mechanism, refusing to overwrite an occupied
  level rather than silently evicting a live order's only record.
- **Stage C** — `uncoveredInventoryFindings` returning early on any non-`running`
  bot (`if (snapshot.state.status !== "running") return [];`), which is why both
  bots were invisible to the one check written to find them.

**None of these depends on any specific quantity.** They are statements about
control flow, verifiable in source and exercised by the suite. Entry 70's Part 8
claim that both bots *"remain COMPLETELY UNTOUCHED and still hold their uncovered
inventory"* is **qualitative and carries no figure**. **Entry 70 stands exactly
as written.**

### Entry 71 — no revision warranted

Entry 71's core conclusion is that **both bots had already self-resolved and
there was nothing left to repair**. That rests on **two live facts, and only

2. `POST /liquidate` returning **`action: "nothing_to_liquidate"`** on both — the

**Neither involves the disputed number in any way.** The conclusion "nothing to

## PART 4 — THE LESSON, STATED HONESTLY

**This is a provenance-labelling gap in how one investigation carried a figure
forward. It is not a bug.**

Stated without hedging:

- **It is not a defect in any system code.** No source file behaved wrongly. The
  Aug-5 comment is accurate about Aug-5; `claimSlot` fixed what it says it fixed.
- **It is not a misunderstanding of any bot's actual behavior.** `bot-4xcq8p`'s
  real history is coherent throughout: two rungs stranded by a collision on
  2026-08-05, the collision fixed the same day, later detections firing and
  resolving on 12/14/17 August, a covering sell that filled, and a flat position
  at the live check.
- **It is not a case of a wrong number being invented.** `0.00037572` is a real,
  correct figure. It was **correctly recorded** — of the **wrong date**.

**The one thing that actually went wrong:** a number sourced from a **source-code
comment describing a past, resolved incident** was carried into a live
investigation and treated, **without an explicit label**, the same way as a
number obtained from a **live D1 query or a live API response** in that same
chain. This project already labels operator-supplied live figures `[OPERATOR]`
with care — entries 70 and 71 both do so rigorously and repeatedly. **What had no
label was the third category: historical figures recovered from the repository
itself.**

**Recorded as process learning worth naming for future investigations in this
project, and nothing more.** No corrective action beyond this record is proposed,
and none is scheduled. The discipline that made this findable was **entry 71
refusing to smooth the discrepancy over** — had it guessed at a reconciliation,
the trail would have been closed with a wrong answer instead of left open with a
correct question.
