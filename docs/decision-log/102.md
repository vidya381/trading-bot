## PART 1 — THE RESUME BUG: A COUNTER THAT SURVIVES THE THING THAT SHOULD RESET IT

### What was observed

`bot-ts1` (trailing stop, BTCUSD, gemini-main testnet) had **15 real orders,
every one of them created between 2026-09-01T21:57 and 22:42Z, and nothing
after**. It had been resumed repeatedly across the following week, and each
resume re-fired a `halt_entry_unfilled` alert — most recently **2026-09-08
20:08:20Z** (15:08 CDT, which is the timestamp an operator would have seen).
Every resume looked like a retry that failed. **None of them was a retry at all.**

### The mechanism, stated plainly

`entryAttempts` (`BotRuntimeState`) counts how many times the trailing stop's
single entry has been PLACED. It is **never reset, by design**, and
`trailing-stop.ts` states the reasoning in the field's own header: *"There is one
entry in this strategy's whole life (22.2 decision 4), so a counter that could be
reset would be counting something else."* That reasoning is correct and is not
disturbed here.

**`#resumePass` clears exactly four fields** — `status`, `haltReason`,
`haltedAt`, `postHaltEvents` — **and spreads everything else through unchanged.**
So `entryAttempts` survives the resume at 3. `#trailingStopOnPrice` then feeds
the carried-over count straight back into `decide`, whose 22.10 gate is checked
**BEFORE** `open_entry` can be returned — deliberately, per its own comment: *"at
the cap, the next answer is the halt, not one more order followed by a halt."*

So `#placeTrailingStopEntry` — the only site that places an entry, and the only
site that increments the counter — **was never reached**. The bot could not place
an order, could not advance the counter, and could not escape.

**⚠ EVERY COMPONENT HERE WAS BEHAVING AS DESIGNED.** The counter was right to
persist. The gate was right to fire. `decide` was right to check the cap before
the placement. **`resume` was the only thing that was wrong**, and it was wrong by
omission — it never asked the question.

### Why this was invisible for a week

Because the failure was **success-shaped at every surface**. `resume` returned
`{status: "running", action: "resumed"}`, wrote a real `bot.resumed` audit row
carrying `previous_halt_reason`, and mirrored `running` to D1. The dashboard
showed a running bot. The re-halt arrived on the next candle as a *separate*
event, and `resolveHaltAlerts` had already closed the previous `halt_entry_unfilled`
row, so each cycle **closed one critical alert and opened a fresh one** — which
reads as a bot repeatedly trying and repeatedly failing, not as a bot that never
tried.

### Confirmed by reproduction, not by inference

Driven end-to-end against **real Durable Object storage and real D1** in the
Workers pool — a throwaway test, since deleted, whose assertions were then
rewritten as the permanent ones described in Part 3:

```
AT HALT:        placed=3  status=halted   entryAttempts=3  openOrderIds=[]
RESUME RESULT:  { status: 'running', action: 'resumed' }
AFTER RESUME:   status=running  entryAttempts=3  haltReason=null
FIRST CANDLE:   { status: 'halted', action: 'halted', detail: "entry_unfilled: ..." }
AFTER TICK:     status=halted  entryAttempts=3  placed=3        ← unchanged
halt_entry_unfilled alert rows: 2  [{resolved: true}, {resolved: false}]
```

The live row agrees with the reproduction exactly: `bot-ts1`'s stored
`halt_reason` reads *"the entry order was placed **3** times and never filled"*.

### The fix

**`resume` now refuses.** A new `BotInstanceErrorCode`, **`entry_budget_spent`**,
thrown from `#resumePass` when a `trailing_stop` bot's `entryAttempts >=
MAX_ENTRY_ATTEMPTS`, mapped to **409** in `src/api/envelope.ts`.

Three properties of the gate, each deliberate:

* **Placed with the other refusals, BEFORE `#subscribeToFeed`.** Everything above
  that line is a pure check; the subscribe is the method's first side effect and
  can bring a whole `PriceFeed` socket back up for a pair whose last subscriber
  left. A refused resume must not do that and then throw.
* **Strategy-gated on `config.strategy === "trailing_stop"`, not a bare field
  read.** The field is `undefined` on DCA and grid, so `?? 0` would make the
  comparison false anyway — the explicit check keeps that true **by construction
  rather than by arithmetic**, so this gate can never refuse a DCA or grid resume.
* **`>=`, not `===`**, matching `decide`'s gate exactly. A count above the cap is
  reachable: the cap was deployed onto live bots that had already placed entries
  under no bound at all.

**⚠ A 409 THAT IS NEVER CLEARED, UNLIKE ITS TWO NEIGHBOURS.** `position_unverified`
and `orders_unresolved` are both conditions someone else resolves, after which
the identical request works — and `envelope.ts`'s existing comments say exactly
that. **That sentence is false here.** Nothing anywhere resets `entryAttempts`.
The status is still right (a state conflict, not a malformed request; and not 403,
which would claim the caller lacks permission when in fact no actor can do this),
but the *message* had to carry what the status cannot: this bot is finished, and
the remedy is a new bot. Both the code comment and the error prose say so.

### WHY REFUSE RATHER THAN RESET THE COUNTER ON RESUME

The obvious alternative — clear `entryAttempts` on resume, so a human review
grants three fresh attempts — **was considered and rejected.**

**It reintroduces the exact unbounded retry the cap was built to stop.** The cap
exists (entry for `0339f90`, and this file's own test header) because a live bot
placed its entry **ten times** at the identical price against a venue that
cancelled every one, and would have continued indefinitely. Resetting on resume
does not remove that loop; it **puts a human in it**. A bot facing a genuinely
uncooperative market or a venue cancelling its orders would burn three fresh
attempts per click, forever, and each click would look like progress. The bound
would no longer be three attempts — it would be three attempts *per resume*,
which is not a bound.

**Refusing keeps the cap meaning what it says**, and moves the decision to where
it belongs: a human deciding whether to create a new bot, having been told why
the last one failed.

### ⚠ WHAT THIS DOES NOT FIX, AND IT IS THE BIGGER QUESTION

**Nothing here explains why Gemini testnet cancelled all 15 of `bot-ts1`'s real
orders.** Each entry was a limit priced to cross the spread — *priced to fill
immediately* — so an order that did not fill was almost certainly cancelled at
the venue rather than left behind by the market. The halt prose has said so since
`0339f90`; it is still a guess, and it is still unverified.

This fix makes the bot **stop lying about retrying**. It does not make the entries
fill, and **a new bot created today would spend its three attempts the same way.**
That investigation is open and untouched — see PART 4.

## PART 2 — THE LIQUIDATION SILENT-FAILURE LOOP

**This is the more serious finding.** Part 1 wasted operator time. This one has
two real bots in a state with **no exit**, and an audit log that says otherwise.

### The two bots

Both grid, BTCUSD, gemini-main, both `halted`, both `archived = 0`, both holding
**$100.00 of allocated capital**:

| Bot | Held base | Buys | Fills | Halted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bot-gnqel3` | **0.00000532 BTC** | 6, all `cancelled` | 1 trade, 532 units | 2026-09-08 20:10:49Z |
| `bot-80inbh` | **0.00000108 BTC** | 8, all `cancelled` | 108 units | 2026-09-06 21:36:09Z |

**Neither has ever placed a sell order. Not one, in either bot's entire history.**

### Where the dust came from

`bot-gnqel3`'s six buys sit at six ladder rungs. Five filled **zero**. The sixth,
`v1-bot-gnqel3-5` at the top rung ($76,247.85), **partially filled 532 of 15,738
units — 3.4% of one rung — and was then cancelled.** One `trades` row, 2026-08-29,
532 units, fee $0.00081.

That partial fill is the entire held position, and it is uncovered because of a
structural gap reconciliation's own halt prose names precisely:

> *Section 6.2's replace-on-fill is the only thing that places a grid sell, and it
> does not re-run for a rung already missed, so this will not correct itself.
> Nothing is queued to place one.*

**A grid buy that partially fills and is then cancelled leaves base with no sell
against it, permanently.** There is no mechanism anywhere in the grid strategy
designed to handle it. `bot-80inbh` is the same story at 108 units.

### THE CRITICAL FINDING: LIQUIDATE DOES NOT FAIL LOUDLY

The held quantity is **below the venue's minimum order size**. Gemini's BTCUSD
`minQty` is **1000 units (0.00001 BTC)**; `bot-gnqel3` holds **532**. The dust is
worth roughly **$0.41**, and the smallest order that can be constructed is about
**$0.78**. It is not sellable — not at a worse price, not in pieces, **not at all.**

`#placeLiquidationSell` **detects this correctly.** `validateOrder` returns
invalid, and the method raises a **critical** `liquidation_not_constructible`
alert saying so. **And then it returns — without throwing.**

**⚠ THE CALLER TREATS THAT RETURN AS SUCCESS, AND IT HAS ALREADY WRITTEN THE
AUDIT ROW.** `#liquidatePositionPass` writes **`bot.liquidated`** — carrying
quantity and price — **BEFORE** calling `#placeLiquidationSell`, and then returns
`{status: "halted", action: "liquidating", detail: "0.00000532"}` **regardless of
what actually happened.** The operator sees a success. The audit log records a
liquidation. **Nothing was ever sent to the exchange.**

**Confirmed in the live audit log: five such rows.** Three on 2026-09-01, and two
more today at **20:13:09Z and 20:13:27Z** — an operator clicking liquidate, twice,
eighteen seconds apart, getting a success both times, and changing nothing. Four
unresolved `liquidation_not_constructible` alerts stand against `bot-gnqel3`
recording what really happened; **the audit log, which is the append-only record
of what this system did, says five liquidations occurred.**

`bot-80inbh` has no such alerts only because **nobody has clicked liquidate on it
yet.** Its position is below the same minimum. It will behave identically.

### THE DEADLOCK, STATED EXPLICITLY

* **Archive refuses.** `assertFlatBeforeRelease` sees `ladder.heldQuantity > 0`
  and throws **409 `position_held`**: *"Liquidate the position first (the bot must
  be halted to do so), then try again."* This is **correct** — archiving releases
  the full $100 allocation as though it were cash, when part of it is inventory.
  No `bot.archived` audit row exists for either bot; the refusal precedes every
  write.
* **Liquidate silently does nothing.** Below the venue minimum, forever, as above.
* **Therefore there is currently no way out of this state for either bot.** The
  remedy each control names is the other control, and one of the two cannot work.
  **$200 of allocation is locked behind $0.49 of unsellable dust.**

### ⚠ TWO REMEDIATION OPTIONS, NEITHER CHOSEN HERE

**This is deliberately left as a decision for a future session, not defaulted
into.** Both options mean **deliberately and explicitly abandoning real base
asset**, which is a human judgement call, and this codebase has repeatedly refused
to make that call automatically — `assertFlatBeforeRelease`'s own header says
*"IT REFUSES; IT NEVER SELLS … A gate that auto-sold to get itself unblocked would
be the one thing every one of those decisions was written to prevent."* A gate
that auto-*wrote-off* deserves the same suspicion.

1. **Write the dust off into `unmodelledBase`.** The mechanism already exists and
   is already used for exactly this class of residue by DCA (`#completeCycle`) and
   the trailing stop (`#completeTrailingStopExit`): base the bot owns, no longer
   counts, and will never trade. Applying it here would be reusing a decided
   pattern rather than inventing one — but those sites accumulate residue at a
   *cycle boundary the bot reached*, and this would be doing it to a bot that
   never got there.
2. **A below-minimum override on the archive gate.** Let `assertFlatBeforeRelease`
   pass when the held quantity is provably unsellable at the venue's own `minQty`,
   with the abandonment recorded. Narrower in blast radius, but it puts a venue
   filter into a capital-safety gate, and the gate would then be trusting
   `filters` — which are cached, and refreshed hourly.

**Not decided. Not implemented.** Recorded so the next session starts from the
two real options rather than rediscovering the deadlock.

### ⚠ MUTATION-TESTED, AND THIS IS THE POINT OF TEST 1

Disabling the gate in `#resumePass` — leaving everything else untouched — makes

The other two are the boundary cases and correctly stay green, since disabling the
gate does not change how a bot below the cap or a DCA bot behaves.

This was run **specifically because a test that only asserted `rejects` would also
pass against a broken implementation** — one that flipped the status, closed the
halt alert, and *then* threw. That is the original bug displaced by one layer, and

and creating one is not yet advisable. Note the same venue-cancellation signature
appears in Part 2's data: `bot-gnqel3`'s six buys and `bot-80inbh`'s eight were

flagged as a question, not a conclusion.

**(b) Decide and implement a remediation path for the two dust-locked bots.**
Untouched. The two options are recorded in Part 2; neither is chosen. Until one
is, `bot-gnqel3` and `bot-80inbh` cannot be liquidated or archived, **and clicking
liquidate on either will continue to write a `bot.liquidated` audit row for a
liquidation that does not happen.**

**⚠ A THIRD ITEM, SMALLER, ALSO NOT DONE:** `#liquidatePositionPass` writing
