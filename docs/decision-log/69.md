### What this is

The close of a defect **found during fix #3's design (entry 59) and deliberately
left unfixed at the time**, with its own accounting written into
`repairPosition`'s findings so it would not be forgotten.

It is not a confirmation. **The investigation found the bug was different from,
and substantially larger than, the thing entry 59 recorded.**

---

## PART 1 — THE ORIGINAL PREMISE, AND WHY IT WAS WRONG

**As originally understood:** `#placeTakeProfitSell` sizes the exit sell by
rounding the position quantity DOWN onto the exchange's step size — the only
safe direction, since rounding up asks to sell more base than the account holds
and the exchange rejects the whole order rather than trimming it.
`#completeCycle` then zeroes the position regardless. The difference was assumed
to be a **sub-lot rounding residue, a few sats per completed cycle.**

**The correction.** `#completeCycle` carried this comment in its own body, added
by fix #5 (entry 60) when the position stopped being decremented wholesale:

> *"The position it zeroes has already been reduced to ZERO by the final fill's
> `applyExit` (a full exit sells exactly what the position held, and the
> remainder-form cost basis reaches exactly zero with it)."*

**That premise is false, and it is the whole bug.** A full exit sells **what the
rounding logic reduced the ORDER to**, not what the position held. `applyExit`
correctly decrements by each fill; the fills sum to the *order's* quantity. The
gap between the order's quantity and the position's is exactly what
`EMPTY_POSITION` silently discarded — on **every** completed cycle, permanently,
visible nowhere.

The comment has been corrected in place rather than left to mislead the next
reader.

---

## PART 2 — TWO DISTINCT SHAPES, NOT ONE, WITH REAL SIZES

Both were reproduced against the real Durable Object before anything was fixed.

### Shape A — step-size rounding. Bounded, and usually ZERO.

`residue = position.quantity mod stepSize`, **strictly less than one step size.**

**And in ordinary operation it is exactly zero.** Every buy is *placed* at a
step-aligned quantity — `validateOrder` floors it at
`src/exchange/binance/filters.ts:383` — and a venue fills a step-aligned order in
step-aligned pieces, so `position.quantity` is already a multiple of the step and
the floor on the exit is a no-op.

It becomes non-zero only when **the grid itself moves underneath a live cycle**:
`#ensureFilters` refetches symbol filters once an hour, and a venue that
**coarsens** `stepSize` mid-cycle strands everything below the new step.
Reproduced by coarsening `stepSize` from `0.00000001` to `0.00001` between the
buy and the exit: **0.00000519 BTC stranded.**

**This is the "a few sats" case — and usually it is not even that. Usually it is
nothing at all.**

### Shape B — a buy folding in after the exit was sized. NOT bounded by anything.

The exit order's quantity is **fixed at placement**. Base acquired afterwards
cannot be in it.

The mechanism, and every step of it is deliberate, correct behaviour:

1. `#placeTakeProfitSell` cancels resting buys before sizing the exit.
2. `#cancelOpenOrders` **keeps a buy in `openOrderIds` when the cancellation
   cannot be CONFIRMED** — section 5.6 forbids treating an unreachable exchange
   as a cancellation — and alerts `cancel_failed`. The buy may still be live.
3. `#recordCancellation` **deliberately defers an under-recorded fill to the next
   poll** rather than synthesising a fill id, because `applyFill` deduplicates on
   the exchange's own id and an invented one either double-counts or is silently
   swallowed.
4. If that deferred fill lands **between the exit's sizing and the cycle's
   completion**, `applyEntry` folds it into the position — and `#completeCycle`
   then discards **the entire buy's worth**.

**Reproduced: 0.0005 BTC discarded in one test case.**

**The "a few sats" framing was an understatement, not an imprecision.** Shape A's
premise was that the loss is bounded by one step size. In the reproduced Shape B
case the loss was **0.0005 against a step size of 0.00000001 — 50,000 times the
bound it was assumed to obey, about 4.7 orders of magnitude** — and **96 times**
the Shape A residue reproduced in this same session. **Shape B is the shape that
actually matters.**

### A second symptom, previously undetected

Found while quantifying the above, and closed by the same fix.

`#completeCycle` computed its reported profit as
`proceeds − Σ position.entries.cost`. That **expensed the cost basis backing coin
the bot still owned.** Meanwhile `realizedGross` — booked per exit fill by
`applyExit` — charges only the basis that actually *left* the position.

So the audit log's reported realized profit and the bot's own live realized
profit figure **could permanently disagree, with no way to determine which was
correct.** Measured on the Shape A reproduction:

| Figure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `bot.cycle_completed` audit `gross_profit` | **1.5846** |
| The bot's own `realizedGross` | **1.896** |
| Difference | **0.3114** |

**0.3114 is exactly the cost basis of the 0.00000519 the bot still held.**

## PART 3 — WHERE IT WENT, AND WHY IT NEVER SELF-CORRECTED

**The leaked amount was inert, not lost to a third party.** It remained real
crypto in the exchange account. Three independent reasons nothing ever noticed:

1. **Balance reconciliation could not see it, and was right not to.** The model is
   a **delta** (`reconcile.ts:1148`): `internal = last exchange balance + this
   system's own recorded trade activity`. The stranded base was **bought via a
   recorded trade and never sold**, so the exchange's reported balance and the
   internal expectation **agree exactly**. No `balance_drift` finding, ever.
2. **Nothing compared a DCA bot's tracked position against its real balance.**
   Reconciliation has exactly one finding kind of that shape —
   `uncovered_held_inventory` — and it is **grid-specific**. DCA has no
   equivalent.
3. **A future cycle could never reabsorb it.** `#placeBuy` sizes from
   `quantityForQuote(quoteAmount, price)` — **quote-driven**. The bot never reads
   its own base-asset balance.

**Conclusion: the leak was permanent and strictly cumulative. It never
self-corrected and never compounded into anything that would raise an alarm.**

## PART 4 — OPTIONS CONSIDERED AND REJECTED

### (a) Carry the leftover forward into the next cycle — REJECTED

**It would stop the bot trading.** `decide` (`src/strategies/dca.ts:551`) reads
`position.quantity <= ZERO` as *"fresh cycle, place the base order"*. A carried
remainder means **no base order ever fires again**. Worse, the new cycle is
immediately judged against the **OLD** cycle's `averageEntryPrice` — so the first
price tick returns `take_profit` or `stop_loss` on a quantity **too small to
construct a valid order from**, which halts.

**And it would have reopened a hazard fix #3 exists to close.** `repairPosition`
(entry 59) infers a bot's true position as **bought-minus-sold over the live
cycle's real orders**. A carried-forward remainder is not in that cycle's orders,
so the repair would read it as an **overstatement** and "correct" an
already-correct position **downward** — the exact class of silent drift entry 59
was built to remove.

### (b) Sell the dust immediately as a follow-up order — REJECTED

**Shape A's residue is by definition below one step size, and `minQuantity` is
never finer than `stepSize` on a real symbol** — so `validateOrder` answers
`rounded_to_zero` or `quantity_below_min`. **It is often literally impossible to
construct the order at all.**

Shape B's residue could sometimes be sold — but that means placing a **new order
inside the cycle-completion path**, with every piece of the fill/cancellation
safety machinery fix #5 (entry 60) already had to build once: what if it rests
unfilled and the cycle cannot complete, what if it part-fills. **Disproportionate
complexity for something that should be reported, not autonomously traded.**

### (c) Visibility only, without changing what is tracked — COLLAPSED INTO THE CHOICE

**Producing an honest number to display requires accumulating it somewhere in the
first place.** Deriving it after the fact means re-walking order history, which
only `repairPosition` does and only on a halted bot in dry-run. **Accumulating it
properly IS the fix**, and the result is never traded — so this option and the
chosen one are the same option done properly.

### (d) CHOSEN — an explicit, named, never-automatically-traded balance

Tracked on the bot, **fed into nothing**: not `decide`, not `autoRestart`, not
any threshold. **Purely bookkeeping and visibility.**

## PART 5 — WHAT WAS BUILT, AND THE INDEPENDENT CROSS-CHECK

### `unmodelledBase`, optional, on `BotRuntimeState`

**Optional, defaulting to ZERO on read**, so every existing bot's stored state
stays valid. **No `schemaVersion` bump** — bumping would trip
`assertReadableSchema` and brick every live bot. This follows **this project's
own established precedent for additive optional state fields: `exitKind` and
`pendingReplacements`**, both documented in place as valid-on-old-state for
exactly this reason.

### Both completion paths accumulate instead of discarding

`#completeCycle` **and** `#completeLiquidation` now add the remainder into
`unmodelledBase` inside the `#mutateState` callback — captured there rather than
from a snapshot read earlier, because the audit and alert that follow are D1
writes and therefore re-entry points, and the figure is a property of the
position **at the instant it is cleared**.

Both also **net the retained basis off `spent`**, which closes the profit-figure
discrepancy from Part 2. On a clean cycle `position.cost` is exactly ZERO
(`applyExit`'s remainder form guarantees it), so **the ordinary path is
bit-identical to before.**

**`#completeLiquidation` was in scope as the same defect under a different
trigger, not an expansion.** `#placeLiquidationSell` sizes through the identical
`validateOrder(..., { rounding: "adjust" })` and the method clears the position
identically. Three lines.

### Reporting

- The **`bot.cycle_completed` audit entry** carries `unmodelled_base` and
  `unmodelled_base_total` **unconditionally, including as an explicit zero** — so
  a healthy cycle is a **recorded fact**, not an absent key that would read
  identically to state written before this field existed.
- A **new `warning`-level alert `cycle_unmodelled_base`**, raised **only when the
  amount is non-zero**, saying plainly that the base is still in the account and
  will not be traded.

### THE CROSS-CHECK — and a correction to how it was framed

**Two independently-derived figures agreeing is much stronger evidence than one
calculation trusted on its own.** That is what was obtained here, but **it must
be recorded accurately, because the mechanism is not what it might be assumed to
be.**

**`repairPosition`'s dust finding was NOT extended by this session. It already
existed** — it was written during fix #3 (entry 59), which found this defect,
declined to fix it, and left the finding behind with a comment stating the
residue *"is a SEPARATE defect, is not part of the live cycle, and is
deliberately not folded into the numbers below."*

**This session did not touch it.** `repairPosition` is unmodified. What was added
is a **test asserting the two agree**: `repairPosition` derives the number by
**walking the bot's real order history and differencing bought against sold per
closed cycle**, trusting no running total; `unmodelledBase` derives it from **the
position at the moment it is cleared**. Completely different code paths, written
in **different sessions for different purposes** — and both produce
**0.00000519** on the same reproduction.

**That the two were authored independently makes the agreement stronger evidence,
not weaker: it cannot be an artefact of a shared assumption made once.**

### Nothing in the trading path reads the new field

**Provable from the mechanism, not asserted.** `decide` takes a `DcaPosition`,
**not the bot's full runtime state** — `unmodelledBase` is not a field it can
see. `autoRestart`, the take-profit target, the stop-loss threshold and the
additional-buy trigger all key off `position.averageEntryPrice` and
`position.quantity`, none of which changed. **A test pins this directly:** after a
cycle that stranded 0.0005, the resumed bot still places a **buy**, sized
`0.00166666` from its quote allocation, with `position.quantity` at ZERO and
`unmodelledBase` untouched.

## PART 6 — GRID: CHECKED FROM SOURCE, NO FIX NEEDED, NO CODE TOUCHED

**Grid DOES produce the same kind of residue.** `#placeLiquidationSell` rounds
down through the **identical** `validateOrder` call as DCA's exit.

**Grid does NOT have the leak**, and the reason is the property entry 63
identified: **grid never wholesale-zeroes its position.**
`#applyGridExitFill` writes `heldQuantity: ladder.heldQuantity - fill.quantity`
and nothing else. Every write to `heldQuantity` across the codebase was checked
and is either `± fillQuantity` or the `ZERO` at ladder creation
(`src/strategies/grid.ts:567`). `#gridExit` explicitly clears `slots` while
**preserving** `heldQuantity`.

**So any residue simply remains, correctly, inside the bot's own visible tracked
balance** — and reconciliation already has a grid-specific check for exactly that
condition: **`uncovered_held_inventory`**, *"a grid bot holds base with no sell
resting against any of it."*

**This was confirmed by reading grid's source in this session, not inferred from
entry 63.** Entry 63 answered a **different and narrower question** — whether a
partially-filled-then-cancelled order could leave grid's position *overstated*.
This is the opposite direction: whether grid's exit **sizing** leaves a residue
*understated*. Both answers are "no", for related but distinct reasons, and the
second was checked on its own terms.

**No grid file was modified.**

## PART 8 — NOT DONE / NOT VERIFIED

- **This fix has NOT been deployed and has NOT been exercised live.** No real bot
  has been observed accumulating or reporting a non-zero `unmodelledBase` through
  an actual completed cycle. Every figure in this entry comes from the local
  test suite.
- **The `cycle_unmodelled_base` alert has never been seen firing on real data.**
  Its severity (`warning`), its text, and whether it is the right volume for a
  real fleet are all unvalidated by anything but tests.
- **Whether any live bot is currently carrying stranded base is unknown**, and
  this fix does **not** backfill it. `unmodelledBase` starts at ZERO for every
  existing bot and counts only cycles completed **after** deploy. Historical
  residue would have to be recovered by running `repairPosition`'s dry-run
  finding against each bot, or read off the exchange by hand.
- **Shape B's frequency in practice is unmeasured.** It requires an unconfirmed
  cancellation, which alerts `cancel_failed` when it happens; no attempt was made
  in this session to count how often that has actually occurred.
- **The claim that `minQuantity` is never finer than `stepSize`** is taken from
  how both exchanges' filters are constructed and from `validateOrder`'s ordering
  of checks. It was **not** verified against a live symbol catalogue.
- **Nothing is committed as of writing.** `HEAD` is `87a1924`.
