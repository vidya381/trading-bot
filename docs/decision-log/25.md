## Step 25: The account-level summary on the bot list
Date: 2026-08-06

### What was built

The main bot-list page had seven count tiles and a table of per-bot figures, and
no answer at all to "what is this account actually doing". Every money number on
it was per-bot; totalling them meant reading ten rows and adding in your head,
in a column where the bots need not even share a currency.

- **`AccountSummary.tsx`** — a second row inside `StatusStrip`, below the counts:
  allocated, in-position, idle, realized (gross), fees paid, unrealized (gross),
  net, open positions, DCA cycles. One group per capital asset.
- **`accountTotals.ts`** — the fold, alongside `derive.ts`. Pure, no React, no
  I/O, exact `bigint` Money at SCALE 8 end to end.
- **Four backend additions** to `botSummary`/`positionOf` (`serialize.ts`) and
  `feesFor` (`handlers.ts`), below.
- **Six tests** in `src/api/api.test.ts`, all mutation-verified. **1581 pass**,
  1575 before.

### Where the fee data actually was, which is the whole shape of this step

The brief asked not to assume, and the assumption would have been wrong in the
expensive direction.

`GET /api/bots` already carried allocation and realized gross. It did **not**
carry fees, and fees are the one figure that cannot be derived from a Durable
Object snapshot at all — they live in D1 `trades`, written per fill by
`#mirrorTrade`. So the honest answer to "is it cheaply available" was **no, it
needs new backend work**, and the interesting part is *which* column.

`trades` has two fee columns and only one of them is summable:

| Column | What it is | Summable? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fee_amount` + `fee_asset` | the fee exactly as the venue charged it | **No.** Binance commonly charges in BNB (section 5.5 rule 1). Summing this adds BNB to USD and calls the result money |
| `fee_reporting_amount` | that fee converted to the bot's `capitalAsset` at the fill's own price | Yes — **but nullable** |

It is nullable on purpose, and that nullability is the load-bearing fact of this
whole feature. `#mirrorTrade` builds a rate lookup that knows only the base
asset's price, so a fee paid in the exchange's own token has no rate available
and **all three** reporting columns are left NULL rather than guessed (step 2
decision 9, enforced by migration 0001's `fee_conversion_all_or_nothing` CHECK).

SQLite's `SUM` skips NULLs **silently**. So `SUM(fee_reporting_amount)` alone
cannot distinguish "this bot paid 4.12 in fees" from "this bot paid 4.12 plus an
unknown amount of BNB" — and the second one, presented as the first, understates
cost and therefore **overstates profit**. `feesFor` is therefore two queries, not
one: the exact sum, and `COUNT(*) WHERE fee_reporting_amount IS NULL`. The count
is not diagnostics. It is what makes the omission representable.

### The four backend additions

Two cost nothing, one costs I/O, one is a rename.

1. **`fees: { reported, unpricedCount }`** — the new work, per bot, in that
   bot's own capital asset. `reported` is documented everywhere it appears as a
   **floor, not a total**.
2. **`lastPrice`** and **`cycleCount`** — both were already sitting in the
   snapshot `botSummary` is handed and simply were not published. Marking a
   position to market from the list previously meant one `/api/bots/:id` fetch
   per bot for a number the list had already read.
3. **`position.cost` on the grid arm.** It was DCA-only. The grid arm published
   a held *quantity* with nothing to value it against, so any "what is the fleet
   holding" total silently omitted every grid bot. Grid's `ladder.heldCost` and
   DCA's `position.cost` are the same thing accumulated the same way — the bare
   notional of what is still held, gross of fees — so grid's was renamed to
   match DCA's rather than the reverse, leaving the field the frontend has read
   since 10.7 exactly where it was.

**`lastPrice` and `cycleCount` are `null` for an orphan, not `0`.** An object
holding no state has not seen a price of zero and has not completed zero cycles;
both are unknown, and a rollup reading them as zeroes would produce a confident
total built on missing data. Pinned by a test.

**Cost, stated rather than buried:** two D1 reads per bot on the endpoint the
dashboard polls every 5 seconds — twenty per poll at ten bots, joining the ten
Durable Object reads already there. Issued inside the *same* per-bot
`Promise.all`, so the added latency is one round-trip and not twenty. The
cheaper alternative — a single account-wide `SUM` over all of `trades`, two
queries flat — was rejected on **correctness, not cost**: one SUM blends amounts
denominated in each bot's own capital asset, and a fleet spanning a USDT account
and a USD one would produce a total in no currency at all, looking entirely
normal.

### Three decisions, taken deliberately

**1. Grouped per capital asset, always.** Today every live bot is on
`gemini-main` and this renders as one unlabelled strip. It still groups, because
the failure it prevents is silent: the first USDT-denominated Binance bot beside
a USD-denominated Gemini one would otherwise produce a blended number in no
currency, and nothing about it would look wrong. More than one asset grows a
heading per asset and an explicit note that totals are never summed across them.

**2. Net is withheld, not caveated.** Three separate things can make it
unknowable — an orphaned bot, a held position with no usable price (section 5.6:
an unusable price is reported, never guessed at), and an unpriced fee. When any
is present `net` is `null` and the tile says exactly what is missing and how
much of it.

This is **not a new rule**. `realizedPnl` in `src/shared/fees.ts` has always
returned a `complete: false` arm carrying `gross` and `fees` but deliberately no
`net`, for the reason written there: *an overstated profit is exactly the error a
trading system must not make quietly*. This mirrors it rather than inventing a
second convention. **Every gross figure stays visible throughout** — the
suppression costs the operator nothing they had before.

**3. Gross and net sit side by side and neither replaces the other,** matching
the per-bot "Realized (gross)" convention from 10.7. There is no bare "P&L" tile
anywhere on the page, deliberately.

### Two findings from reading the capital ledger, neither of which was in the brief

**`allocated` must exclude `stopped` bots, and this is not tidiness.**
`releaseBotCapital` (capital/ledger.ts) subtracts a closed bot's allocation from
the ledger's `total_allocated` but reads `bot.allocated_capital` to do it and
**leaves the column on the row** as the historical record. So summing
`allocatedCapital` across every bot reports capital already returned to the
account as still committed — an overstatement that grows monotonically with
every bot ever closed. It would have looked correct on day one and drifted
forever after.

**Idle capital can be legitimately negative, so it is not clamped.** `close`
cancels open orders and releases the capital but does **not** flatten the
position (`#closePass`, verified in source — liquidation is a separate human
decision). A stopped bot can therefore still hold inventory: its cost basis
counts toward in-position, its released allocation does not, and idle goes
negative. That is the one figure that reveals the situation, so it renders
honestly — in amber rather than red, because red reads as "a loss" everywhere
else on this dashboard and this is not one.

### Tests

Six, in `src/api/api.test.ts`'s `bots` block, each mutation-verified against the
mutation it exists to catch:

| Test | Mutation it caught |
| --- | --- |
| fee total + unpriced count | `unpricedCount` forced to 0 → also caught by the list/detail agreement test |
| exact past 2^53 | `sumMoney` reimplemented as a JS float sum |
| grid publishes cost basis | the `cost` line removed from the grid arm |
| lastPrice + cycleCount in the LIST | `lastPrice` dropped from the summary |
| orphan reports null, not zero | `null` → `0` and `null` → `"0.00000000"` |
| detail agrees with list | — |

Two are worth their space specifically. The **fee test drives real fills through
`applyMissedFills`**, so `#mirrorTrade`'s actual section 5.5 conversion runs: a
USDT fee (the capital asset, converts at ONE) and a BNB fee (no rate, all three
columns NULL). It asserts `0.25`, **not** `0.325` — the sum that would have added
two currencies — and `unpricedCount: 1`. The **precision test** uses fees of
`1000000000.00000001` and `0.00000001`; the exact total is
`1000000000.00000002` and a float total is `1000000000.00000000`, confirmed by
running it. Money columns are `INTEGER` in SQLite and `sumMoney` reads
`CAST(SUM(col) AS TEXT)`, so the total never becomes a float at any point.

**The frontend fold has no tests**, because the dashboard has no test runner —
`vitest.config.ts` runs only the Worker suite in the Workers pool and
`dashboard/src` contains no `*.test.*` at all. Standing one up is its own step
and was explicitly deferred. `accountTotals.ts` was kept pure and React-free so
that step can pick it up unchanged, and its behaviour was verified for this
session by bundling it with esbuild and running eight scenarios off-tree:
healthy, unpriced fee, missing price, orphan, stopped-still-holding, mixed
assets, exactness past 2^53, and empty. All eight behaved as designed — including
the negative-idle and asset-grouping cases above, which is where that check
earned itself. **That is a one-off run, not a regression guard**, and this entry
should not be read as claiming the module is covered.

### Not fixed here

- **The dashboard test runner.** Deferred deliberately as its own step. Until it
  exists, every rule in `accountTotals.ts` — net suppression, per-asset grouping,
  the stopped-bot exclusion — is held in place only by its docblock and by the
  backend contract tests underneath it. The backend tests pin the *inputs*;
  nothing pins the fold.
- **`unpricedCount` says how many fills, not how much.** A bot with one unpriced
  0.0001 BNB fee and a bot with two hundred of them both merely suppress the net
  figure. Quantifying the gap needs a rate the system deliberately does not have
  (that refusal is step 2 decision 9 and is correct); what could be shown is the
  raw `fee_amount` totals grouped by `fee_asset`, which is real information
  rather than a guess. Not built — the account whose stakes justify it does not
  exist yet, and a partial fee figure in a *second* denomination on the main page
  is its own way to be misread.
- **`cycleCount` is DCA-only and the tile says so** ("DCA cycles"), rather than
  being filtered to DCA bots. Summed as reported, so the figure stays correct if
  grid ever gains a cycle notion; only the label would need revisiting. Under a
  bare "Cycles" heading a grid bot's permanent 0 reads as having achieved
  nothing, which is why the qualifier is in the label and not in a hint.
