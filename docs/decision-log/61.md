## PART 1 — THE BUG THIS CLOSES

Two observers watch the same order at different cadences:

- the owning bot's own poll (`#pollOpenOrders`), every **30 seconds**;
- reconciliation, every **5 minutes**, through `liveOrderFindings` → `driftAgainst`.

For an order that had **gone from the book**, reconciliation already forgave the
gap between them: a 60-second `timingWindowMs` treats a recent termination as
`order_recently_terminated`, minor, in its own words *"a late fill, not drift;
the bot's own fill path will record it."*

**For an order still ON the book there was no equivalent tolerance at all.** Any
disagreement between local and remote fill count became `order_state_drift`,
floor `meaningful` — which halts the bot — **even though the bot's own poll would
usually apply that exact fill, by its real id, within thirty seconds.**

That asymmetry is what produced the false-alarm halts on `bot-b23y63` and
`bot-bl4e7c`. Reconciliation caught a real, temporary, self-resolving
disagreement and halted the bot before the other observer got its turn — and, on
the code as it stood then, that halt was the step that made the damage permanent.

## PART 2 — THE INVESTIGATION

### Q1 — a shared comparison function, and the coupling that turned out to be separable

`driftAgainst` is **shared** by both branches: the live branch called it with no
context, the terminated branch with `"it is no longer on the book"`. The brief
asked me to stop and report if the two could not be separated.

They can. **The 60-second window sits OUTSIDE the shared function** — it is an
early `continue` in the terminated branch, evaluated before `driftAgainst` is
ever reached. So the live-branch tolerance could be added without touching the
window at all.

**The deliberate choice was to parameterise rather than duplicate.** `driftAgainst`
gained a `kind` parameter defaulting to `order_state_drift`, leaving the
terminated caller behaviourally identical. The alternative — a second copy of the
function for the live branch — is **two independently-worded comparisons of the
same two quantities**, which is precisely the drift entry 57 spent an entry
refusing to introduce. A test asserts the terminated branch still reports real
drift on the first run.

### Q2 — reconciliation is structurally blind to the other observer

Stated as a real architectural finding rather than an assumption: **this job
cannot see the poll.** `BotSnapshot` carries `config`, `state` and `orders`, and
the poll's `PollSchedule` lives under its own storage key inside the Durable
Object. Nothing reconciliation has access to can tell it when a bot last polled,
when it will poll next, or whether its poll is even healthy.

That rules out the most natural-sounding tolerance ("forgive if the poll is about
to run") outright, and is why the mechanism below reads history instead.

### Q3 — the memory is `audit_log`, not a new table

Every run starts fresh, so persistence had to come from somewhere. It came from
what was already being written: **every reconciliation run already records its
complete findings list to `audit_log.details_json`** — the fact
`/src/alerts/standing.ts` names as the reason the alerts table may be
deduplicated at all. `unconfirmedDriftFromRecentRuns` reads that back.

No new table, no new column on an existing one, no new alert type.

**Matching is STRUCTURAL, not prose.** `Finding` gained a real `clientOrderId`
field and the audit payload gained `client_order_id`. Matching an order by
searching the `detail` sentence would have put a safety decision on text nothing
tests and any reword defeats — **the exact trap entry 58 identified and refused**
when it declined to recognise a reconciliation halt by string-matching
`haltReason`. Repeating it one entry later would have been worse than not knowing
about it.

### Q4 — the first sighting gets its own kind, and it cannot escalate on its own

`order_drift_unconfirmed`, with **`TIER_FLOOR` and `TIER_CEILING` both `minor`.**

Minor raises no alert row and halts nothing, while still being written to the
run's findings list — so the mechanism that RECORDS is the same one that
REMEMBERS. The ceiling is **pinned** deliberately: this kind means "not yet
confirmed", so letting magnitude promote it would allow a large first-sighting
disagreement to halt a bot on exactly the evidence the kind exists to call
insufficient. It carries no magnitude today; the pin makes that a property of the
kind rather than of the call site.

### Q5 — THE SAFETY PROPERTY: this delays a false alarm, it cannot silence a real one

**Escalation on a second sighting is UNCONDITIONAL.** No severity check, no poll-
health check, no retry budget, no back-off. If the same order still disagrees on
a later run inside the window, it becomes `order_state_drift` and the bot halts.

So the worst case for a **genuine** persistent drift is that it halts **one
reconciliation cycle (~5 minutes) later than before** — never indefinitely
delayed, never conditional on anything that could fail open.

**The 600-second window has a specific job, and it is a safety job rather than a
tuning knob.** Section 5.6 makes a run that could not reach the exchange skip
order reconciliation entirely, recording no finding for an order it never saw.
Without a window that spans more than one turn of the five-minute cron, such a
run would silently reset the count back to "first sighting" — and a venue
flapping between readable and unreadable could hold a real drift at first
sighting forever. Two turns of the cron is the bound that closes that. Twenty of
the owning bot's own 30-second poll passes fit inside it.

**Both bounds are tested, by name:** `DOES halt once the same disagreement
survives a second run` (the escalation) and `does not escalate on an unconfirmed
sighting older than the window` (the bound).

### The Gemini `updatedAt` gap — FOUND, NOT FIXED, and correctly deferred

The existing 60-second terminated-order tolerance is already inoperative on
Gemini, because `parseOrderStatus` sets `updatedAt = createdAt` (Gemini sends no
last-update time), so `age` measures the order's total age rather than time since
termination.

**It was not folded into this fix, and the reason is that it is not a wiring
problem.** Gemini does not supply the information, and both available
substitutions make the EXISTING tolerance worse in opposite directions:

- **order-creation time** (today): `age` is the order's whole life, so the window
  **never** applies to any Gemini order older than a minute;
- **receipt time**: `age` is always ≈ 0, so the window **always** applies — which
  would silence real drift on **every** terminated Gemini order. Strictly worse
  than the status quo.

It needs a different staleness signal entirely, which is its own piece of work,
not a small clearly-scoped correction. Deferred, per the brief's instruction to
report rather than expand scope.

**This fix does NOT inherit that problem.** The tolerance built here is founded on
run-to-run memory and reads no timestamp from the venue at all, so it is
**venue-independent by construction** and behaves identically on Gemini and
Binance.

## PART 3 — WHAT WAS BUILT

- **`order_drift_unconfirmed`** — a new `FindingKind`, floor `minor`, ceiling
  pinned to `minor`.
- **`unconfirmedDriftFromRecentRuns`** — reads recent `reconciliation.run` audit
  rows for the account, bounded by `unconfirmedWindowMs` (600s, two turns of the
  five-minute cron) and capped by `RECENT_RUN_SCAN`, returning the set of
  `botInstanceId::clientOrderId` pairs already seen as unconfirmed.
- **`driftAgainst` parameterised by `kind`** rather than duplicated, defaulting to
  `order_state_drift` so the terminated caller is unchanged.
- **`Finding.clientOrderId` and `client_order_id` in the audit payload**, so the
  cross-run match is on a field rather than on a sentence.

The live branch now reports a first sighting as unconfirmed and only escalates
when the same order was already seen inside the window.

## PART 4 — VERIFICATION, REAL NUMBERS

### Five tests added

1. **`does NOT halt on the first sighting: the false alarm that halted two real bots`**
   — the exact shape. Asserts the finding is `order_drift_unconfirmed` at tier
   `minor`, that no `order_state_drift` is produced, `haltedBotIds` is empty, the
   bot row is still `running`, zero
   `reconciliation_meaningful_order_state_drift` rows exist — and that the run's
   own audit row carries the finding with `client_order_id` set structurally.
2. **`DOES halt once the same disagreement survives a second run`** — Q5's safety
   property, tested rather than argued: meaningful tier, bot halted, one alert
   row, message matching `/still disagreeing on a later run/`.
3. **`forgives again once the bot's own poll has caught up`** — the other half of
   "delays, not silences": a disagreement that DOES self-resolve never reaches
   the halting tier, however many runs follow.
4. **`does not escalate on an unconfirmed sighting older than the window`.**
5. **`leaves the TERMINATED-order branch exactly as it was`** — asserted, not
   assumed, because `driftAgainst` is shared: the terminated case still reports
   real drift on the FIRST run and still halts.

## PART 6 — CLOSING THE CHAIN

**All five fixes are now built.** The chain began at step 56: while the operator
was testing the new archive gate's refusal path, `bot-b23y63` turned out to be
**stuck** — halted, holding a real position, unable to liquidate — and entry 56
recorded that as found, not investigated. Investigating it produced a second
stuck bot and a five-part defect.

The end state, stated without overclaiming. **Permanent damage on cancellation is
prevented** — a halt's cancel sweep can no longer close an order at a fill count
it knows is behind the exchange (entry 57). **Resuming into known-bad state is
refused** — `resume()` will not re-enter `running` while an order-state-drift
alert stands for that bot (entry 58). **The two originally-affected bots were
repaired live and verified by a real completed trade** — 0.00043085 BTC sold at
68169.88, with step 56's archive gate then independently agreeing the bot was
flat (entry 59). **The underlying miscalculation is closed for all future bots** —
exit fills now decrement the position as they land, so the state those two bots
reached can no longer be produced (entry 60). And **false alarms no longer act
immediately** — a live-order disagreement is given one reconciliation cycle to
resolve itself, while any GENUINE persistent drift still halts the bot, at most
five minutes later than it would have (this entry).

Two findings were deliberately deferred and are the honest remaining edges of
this work rather than failures of it: **the Gemini `updatedAt` gap**, which needs
a staleness signal the venue does not supply, and **the dust leak**, which is a
separate accounting defect that folding into any of these fixes would have made
one number wrong in a new way while fixing another. Both are recorded, neither is
scheduled. **Grid remains uninvestigated**, and that is the largest genuinely
unknown edge left.
