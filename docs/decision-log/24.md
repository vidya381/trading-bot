## Step 24: A severe-tier trip investigated, the silence that preceded it, and a live balance-arithmetic bug found while assessing the follow-up
Date: 2026-08-05

Three findings, in the order they surfaced: an investigation that corrected its
own premise, the observability gap that hid the incident's middle, and — found
only because the deferred open question was assessed properly rather than filed
— a double-count that was mis-measuring every pass on the one account that has
bots.

### The question, and the premise that was wrong

Asked to investigate a **2267-unit unexplained increase in `0G` for
`gemini-main`**, severe tier, and to find out whether it tripped the
account-wide circuit breaker and halted any bots.

It tripped the breaker. It halted nothing. And it was never `gemini-main`.

Every one of the **1,359** `0G` rows in `balance_snapshots` carries
`account_label = 'main'` — the **Binance** account. There has never been a `0G`
row under `gemini-main`. That account's most recent pass holds six assets (BCH,
BTC, ETH, LTC, USD, ZEC), all `minor`, and it has no `circuit_breakers` row at
all, which per migration 0003 is equivalent to `armed`.

Worth stating plainly, because step 16's incident makes the confusion
reasonable: `0G` under a **Gemini** label was step 16's bug, and it was fixed
(commit `293f4cb`). `0G` under the **Binance** label is not a bug. It is that
account's real testnet holding. The asset itself is a genuine token — 0G Labs /
Zero Gravity, CoinGecko `zero-gravity`, market-cap rank ~653 — not a promotional
credit or a sandbox invention. What is synthetic is the *quantity*: it comes
from the Binance testnet faucet, which also issues `2Z`, `1MBABYDOGE` and a row
of mock tokenised equities (`AAPLB`, `NVDAB`, `AMZNB`). A real ticker holding
fake units.

The lesson from step 16 repeats in a new shape. There, absence of output was
read as absence of execution. Here, an unfamiliar asset name was read as the
signature of a known bug. Both times the account label was the thing that
actually needed checking.

The trip itself is `circuit_breakers` row `main`, `tripped_at`
`1785957338584`, `run_id` `12ec9351-39e9-4b21-b187-cc3f74c0ff93`, reason field
**75,397 characters** long, with one `circuit_breaker.tripped` audit entry and
one unresolved `critical` alert.

**No bot was halted, and the audit entry says so directly: `"halted":[]`.** Not
because the sweep failed — because `main` has **zero** bot instances. All ten
bots are on `gemini-main` and were untouched. The two that are halted have
halt reasons from 2026-08-03 (meaningful order-state drift) and 2026-08-04
(breakout take-profit), both predating this.

So the severe tier fired at full strength against an empty account. The control
worked; there was simply nothing on the other side of it. Had a Binance bot
existed, it would have been halted correctly — and wrongly, in the sense that
the underlying event was a faucet, not drift.

Every individual finding was **arithmetically correct**. `0G`: exchange 3569.0,
last observation 1302.0, own recorded activity 0.0, adjustments 0.0 → 2267.0
unexplained, and 2267/3569 is 64% of the reference, far past any threshold.
`balance_drift` is the one kind whose `TIER_CEILING` reaches `severe`. Nothing
in the classification path misbehaved.

### The reset

`main` was re-armed at `1785962372974` by the operator, with the cause recorded
in the note (`circuit_breaker.reset` in `audit_log`, plus the `info` alert
`resetAccountCircuitBreaker` writes).

Applied as a direct `wrangler d1 execute` against the testnet database rather
than through `POST /api/circuit-breakers/main/reset`, because that route
requires a Cloudflare Access JWT that cannot be minted outside a browser
session. The write reproduces `resetAccountCircuitBreaker` exactly — the row
update, the audit entry, the info alert — and the note says that it did, so the
provenance is in the record rather than only in a commit message. Same class of
manual operation as the step 11 provisioning steps, for the same reason
(`Repository` exposes no `delete`, section 8.7).

The `circuit_breaker_tripped` alert is deliberately left **unresolved**: the
reset function does not close it either, and the incident's history belongs on
the dashboard beside the re-arm.

### The real defect: an empty balance set was indistinguishable from nothing to check

The four passes at 18:55–19:10 are the finding worth keeping. They were not
blind. `getAccountBalances` **succeeded** and returned a set with no non-zero
balance, and `reconcileBalances` did this with it:

```ts
const assets = new Set<string>([...ledger assets, ...non-zero balances]);
if (assets.size === 0) return [];
```

An early return with no snapshot, no `skipped` entry, no alert, no audit trace.
By every signal the system emits, those were clean passes. "The exchange says
you hold nothing" and "there is nothing here to check" arrived down one code
path, and only the second is benign.

Two consequences, the second worse than the first:

1. The state transition — an account observed with 445 assets seven hours
   earlier now reporting none — was invisible.
2. `resolveClearedAlerts` is gated on `skipped.length === 0`. Those four passes
   therefore counted as **observations**, and were entitled to close live
   standing alerts on the strength of having seen nothing at all. That is
   precisely the failure section 5.6 exists to prevent, reached through a branch
   that never touched the section 5.6 code.

Blindness alerting did not cover it either. `auditBlindness` measures the age of
the last `balance_snapshots` row, so it would eventually have fired — but only
after 16 minutes, and only saying "unobserved", which is the wrong diagnosis for
a pass that read successfully. In this incident it was already open from the
502s, so the empty-set episode contributed nothing new to it.

#### The discriminator, and the one that cannot work

The obvious guard — alert when the account has `capital_ledger` expectations —
**cannot ever fire**, and this is worth recording because it is the first thing
anyone would reach for. `assets` is the union of ledger assets and non-zero
balances, so an empty union already *proves* there are no ledger rows. The
condition would be dead code. It would also have missed this incident exactly:
`main` has no ledger rows at all (the only row in the table is
`gemini-main`/`USD`).

What separates the two cases is **prior observation**. An account seen holding
445 assets and now holding none has undergone a state change. An account never
seen holding anything has not. That is one indexed query, run only in the branch
that is already returning early.

#### `auditEmptyBalanceSet`

Raise-and-clear in one function, in the shape `auditBlindness` already uses, and
in `reconcile.ts` because that is the only place the condition is knowable — the
worker sees the pass's output, not the exchange's response.

- **`reconciliation_empty_balance_set`**, `critical` / `system`, account-scoped
  source `reconciliation:{account}`. Deliberately **not** the blindness type:
  "could not read" and "read, and was told nothing is there" need different
  responses from an operator, and the message names the two causes worth
  checking first — a venue mid-transition, or a client authenticated against the
  wrong account.
- Both halves through `/src/alerts/standing.ts`, with `owns` scoped to this one
  type so the shared source cannot let this lifecycle close
  `reconciliation_blind`'s rows or vice versa.
- It also pushes onto **`skipped`**, which is the fix that actually protects
  something: the empty set is recorded as *unread* rather than as read-and-empty,
  so the baseline survives, no asset is recorded as having drained to zero, and
  the pass stops counting as an observation for alert-resolution purposes.
- An account with no prior observation still returns silently. That case the
  original early return had right.

#### Tests

Six, in `reconcile.test.ts`, replaying the recorded sequence above at the shape
that matters rather than its full 504-asset width — 0G at its real 1302 → 3569.

Five are mutation-verified against two separate mutations: disabling the call
fails four of them, and removing the never-observed guard fails the fifth

assertion.

### The second defect in the same function: lifetime activity, double-counted

Found while assessing whether the stale-baseline gap above was worth building
for. It is a distinct bug, in the same function, and it was live on the account
that actually has bots — so the two must not be read as one issue.

`recordedActivity` opened its window at the account's **oldest** snapshot:

```ts
orderBy: [{ column: "checked_at", direction: "asc" }], limit: 1   // OLDEST
const since = oldest[0]?.checked_at ?? 0;
```

It then returned one scalar sum per asset, and `reconcileBalances` added that
sum to the **most recent** baseline — which already contained every one of those
trades. So `internal_calculated_balance` was the baseline plus the account's
entire trading history, and the reported discrepancy was that history, negated,
on every pass.

The function's own docblock claimed the opposite ("Trades before an asset's own
baseline are excluded by the caller's arithmetic"). The caller did no such
filtering. **The code and its documentation disagreed, and the documentation was
describing the intended design** — which is the useful clue: this was a
regression from a correct idea, not a misconception.

**Confirmed against live data before changing anything.** `gemini-main`, most
recent pass at the time:

| asset | `internal − exchange` | lifetime net activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BTC | `0.00149818` | `+0.00149818` |
| USD | `−96.24364895` | `−(95.7313 quote + 0.51237 fees)` = `−96.24367` |

Agreement to eight decimal places across two assets, from an independent
reconstruction of all 18 trades. And it was a **ratchet**, stepping up on every
buy and back down on every sell as inventory turned over:

```
08-04 18:30   +20.04  (0.0201%)
08-05 06:50   +60.19  (0.0603%)
08-05 14:10   +72.22  (0.0724%)
08-05 15:25   +60.19  (0.0603%)   <- a sell gave some back
08-05 17:40   +96.24  (0.0965%)
```

`meaningfulPct` is 0.1%. USD sat at **0.0965% — 96.5% of the threshold**, about
$3.53 of net spend from crossing it, against observed buy notionals of $12–40.
The next fill would have done it.

The consequence of crossing was checked rather than assumed: `balance_drift` is
account-scoped with `botInstanceId: null`, and `act` halts nothing in that case
("alert only, deliberately"). So the cost was one permanent false critical alert
that could never clear — damage to the alert channel's credibility, not to
trading. `severePct` (2%, ≈$1,995) would trip the breaker and halt all ten bots,
and was **not** near-term: net quote is bounded by deployed inventory against
$1,550 of total allocation (1.55% maximum). It becomes reachable the moment
allocation passes ~$2k or the USD balance falls — neither of which anyone would
have connected to this bug when it happened.

#### The fix

Contributions are now kept **with their timestamps** and summed per asset by
`activitySince(activity, asset, baseline.checked_at)`, so each asset's window
opens at the snapshot this pass is actually subtracting from. One trades query
still serves every asset: no asset's window can open earlier than the account's
oldest snapshot, so that bound was never wrong as a FETCH bound — only as a SUM
bound.

The `attributed` flag became per-contribution for the same reason. It was
per-asset with no timestamp, so one unattributable trade suppressed that asset's
findings for the rest of the account's life; now it poisons only the windows
that actually contain it.

**Why this was safe to deploy directly**, unlike step 16's dispatch fix: it
drives discrepancies toward zero rather than away from them. Verified against
live data first — consecutive `exchange_reported_balance` values for
`gemini-main` were identical and no trade had settled since 17:35, so the
corrected pass computes `internal = baseline + 0 = exchange`, discrepancy zero.
There is no first-corrected-pass hazard here and nothing needed invalidating.

**Four tests**, all mutation-verified against restoring the lifetime window.
They need an OLDER snapshot, a trade after it, and then the baseline that
already reflects that trade — with a single snapshot, oldest and baseline are
the same row and the bug is invisible, which is exactly why all 52 existing
tests in the file passed both before and after the fix. A bug that survives a
suite this thorough survives because of what the fixtures never set up.

Suite: **1575 pass**, 1571 before.

### Not fixed here

- **The stale-baseline comparison itself.** After any long read outage,
  reconciliation compares the first successful read against a baseline of
  arbitrary age, and a differential control has no way to tell "seven hours of
  unobserved drift" from "seven hours of nothing followed by a faucet". Step 16
  identified the general form of this (a differential check cannot detect a
  constant error in its own input); this is its sibling in the time dimension.

  **Deferred deliberately, and the obvious remedy is a trap.** The first draft
  of this entry proposed a baseline that EXPIRES — refusing to accuse against an
  observation older than some multiple of the cron interval, and adopting
  instead. That is written down here because it is what anyone would reach for,
  and it is wrong in the expensive direction. Adopt-on-expiry converts a false
  positive into a **false negative**: an account drained during a seven-hour
  blind window would have the drained balance adopted silently, which is
  precisely the outcome section 5.6 exists to prevent. Accusing loudly against a
  stale baseline is noisy; adopting quietly loses money.

  What is probably right is to keep accusing and attach the STALENESS to the
  finding — so the human reading it sees "this covers a seven-hour unobserved
  window" and can weigh it — rather than to suppress the finding. That is a
  design decision about how a control communicates uncertainty, not a patch, and
  it should wait for an account whose stakes justify the thinking. Recorded, not
  built, and specifically **not** to be built by reading the expiry sentence
  above literally.
- **The severe tier's blast radius on a faucet-backed testnet account.** 256
  severe findings in one pass is not a meaningful signal; it is one event
  reported 256 times. The breaker treats them identically to one real drift.
- **Step 16's open question 1 still stands.** Nothing outside this Worker
  notices if the cron stops firing. `auditEmptyBalanceSet` narrows what "the
  pass ran and saw nothing" can hide, but it is still run by the cron it would
  have to report on.
