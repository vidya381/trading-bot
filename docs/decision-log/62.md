## Step 62: AVAILABLE capital on the bot list — the subtraction the operator was doing by hand, made a real dashboard figure

Date: 2026-08-21 · **NOT COMMITTED** as of writing. Ten files sit on disk: eight
modified, two new.

A read-only display feature. **No capital-mutating code was touched** — not
`adjustAllocated`, not `createBotInstanceWithCapital`, not `releaseBotCapital`,
not `resizeBotCapital`, not reconciliation. Nothing in this step reserves,
releases, or checks capital; it reads two columns and shows their difference.

---

## PART 1 — THE GAP THIS CLOSES

The bot list's stat row showed **ALLOCATED** — committed capital, excluding
stopped bots — and nothing at all telling the operator what capital was actually
**free to use for a new bot**. Answering that question required reading
`capital_ledger.total_balance` and `capital_ledger.total_allocated` out of the
database and subtracting them by hand, every time.

The formula, stated plainly:

```
available = total_balance - total_allocated
```

**This is not a new calculation, and this step invented no arithmetic.** That
subtraction already exists verbatim in three places in this codebase:

- as the **binding capital-availability gate** inside
  `createBotInstanceWithCapital` (`src/capital/ledger.ts`), which refuses with
  `insufficient_capital` when it does not cover the request;
- as `AllocationResult.availableAfter` and
  `AllocationAuditDetails.available_after`, written into `audit_log` on every
  allocation change;
- as `AssetHeadroom.available` in `src/research/capital.ts`.

`research/capital.ts` says it in its own words, and the wording is the reason
this step could proceed without re-deriving anything:

> `total_balance - total_allocated` is not a risk check, it is the definition of
> the word "available".

So the figure now on screen is precisely the figure that decides whether a bot
can be created. That is what makes it worth showing rather than making a human
compute it.

**An investigation preceded the build and is the reason it was safe.** The
question asked first was whether any THIRD bucket of capital exists — something
reserved, pending, or committed but not yet inside `total_allocated` — that a
plain subtraction would miss. It does not:

- **Only `src/capital/ledger.ts` ever writes `total_allocated`**, and every write
  funnels through one function, `adjustAllocated`.
- The **reserve-before-write ordering rule** (`ledger.ts`: "grow the reservation
  BEFORE the bot row, shrink it AFTER") means every in-flight reservation is
  **already inside `total_allocated`** before the bot row exists. Partial
  failures leave capital over-reserved, never under-reserved.
- The **research/proposal pipeline reserves nothing** — `migrations/0009_proposals.sql`
  has no capital column, and `capital.ts` states it "writes no row, takes no
  reservation, touches no `total_allocated`, and holds no lock".
- `seedPlaceholderTotalBalance` never touches `total_allocated` on either path.

## PART 2 — TWO REAL CAVEATS, AND WHY NEITHER BREAKS THE FORMULA

Neither required a design change, and neither is a bug. Both are properties of
what the two columns **mean**, and both are recorded here because a future reader
who finds the number slightly off should read this before hunting for an
arithmetic error. They are also written into code comments at `listAccounts`
(`src/api/handlers.ts`) and restated in `dashboard/src/availableCapital.ts`.

**A. It can OVERSTATE headroom, briefly.** `reconcileBalances` folds
`manual_adjustments` into `total_balance` only when it consumes them — it queries
`reconciled_at: null` and marks the rows in the same batch. A withdrawal logged
through `POST /api/manual-adjustments` is therefore **invisible to this figure
until the next reconciliation run**, and headroom reads high until then.

**B. It can UNDERSTATE headroom.** Reconciliation writes `total_balance` as the
exchange's `free + locked` **for that one asset**. Once a bot spends quote
currency on inventory, that cash has left `total_balance` while the bot's full
reservation still sits in `total_allocated` — so the deployed portion is
subtracted twice, and headroom reads low by that amount.

The two push in **opposite directions**. Neither changes the subtraction.

**This is exactly why the tile's subtext does not claim to be spendable cash.**
The shipped constant is:

```
AVAILABLE_HINT = "balance − allocated; what a new bot can reserve"
```

"what a new bot can reserve" is the claim that is **precisely true** — it is the
arithmetic the creation gate itself runs. "cash available" would have been a
claim caveats A and B both falsify.

> **Character note, recorded because it will otherwise trip a future diff:** the
> subtext uses **U+2212 MINUS SIGN (`−`)**, not an ASCII hyphen. That matches the
> existing Net tile's `"realized − fees + unrealized"` rather than introducing a
> second convention into the same row. Prose elsewhere (including the brief for
> this step) writes it as `-`; the source does not.

## PART 3 — THE THREE DECISIONS MADE BEFORE BUILD

All three were settled by the operator before any code was written, after the
investigation above was reported back.

**1. Data source — extend `GET /api/accounts`, reusing `readAccountCapital`.**
No new endpoint. `readAccountCapital` (`src/research/capital.ts`) already
performs exactly this read, already documents why `available` may be negative,
already orders its rows, and is already tested. Extending the existing
account-totals endpoint reuses tested code instead of minting a second path to
the same two columns. The trade accepted: one ledger read per account,
sequentially. The account registry is human-sized (section 4.4), and the
alternative — one unfiltered `findMany` regrouped in the API layer — would be
this layer restating the read that module owns, which is the duplication being
avoided.

**2. Multi-account keying — one card per `(account, asset)`, never summed.**
`capital_ledger` is keyed `(account_label, asset)` and
`createBotInstanceWithCapital` draws from exactly **one** of those rows. Two
accounts each holding 50,000 USD do not give you 100,000 USD to spend on one bot;
they give you two separate 50,000 limits. A blended total would look completely
normal on screen and be **unspendable** — the same trap `accountTotals.ts`
already documents for blending USD with USDT, one key wider. **Tested explicitly
with a two-account fixture even though today's live data has exactly one
account**, at both layers, because "never summed" is the property most likely to
be broken by a future refactor and least likely to be noticed.

**3. Over-allocated display — negative, amber, unclamped.** When
`total_allocated > total_balance`, the negative figure is shown, coloured amber,
with the subtext swapped to `"over-allocated: allocated exceeds balance"`. It is
**not** clamped to zero and **not** hidden. Precedent and reasoning:

- **Migration 0001 deliberately has no `CHECK (total_allocated <= total_balance)`**,
  with the reason written into the migration itself: a losing position, or funds
  moved manually on the exchange, can legitimately drive a balance below what is
  already allocated, and "an over-allocated account is a real state
  reconciliation must be able to record and alert on, not one the database should
  refuse to represent".
- `AssetHeadroom` in `research/capital.ts` says clamping "would hide an
  over-allocated account from the one report a human reads before allocating
  more".
- **Amber, not red**, and that follows the existing **IDLE** tile exactly, for
  IDLE's own stated reason: red reads as "a loss" everywhere else on this
  dashboard, and this is not one. It wants attention, not alarm.

**ALLOCATED was not touched** — same sum over polled bot rows, same
`"committed; excludes stopped"` subtext, same position.

## PART 4 — TWO EXTENSIONS BEYOND THE LITERAL BRIEF

Both are deliberate engineering judgment taken during the build, recorded as
such. Neither was requested; both are stated here so the operator can reverse
either.

**1. `readAccountCapital` throws, and unhandled that would have taken bot
creation offline.** It raises `ResearchCapitalError("ledger_unreadable")` on a
failed read rather than returning a failure value. Left to escape, that would
have **500'd the entire `GET /api/accounts` endpoint** — which is the create-bot
form's account dropdown and its only source — in order to protect one display
tile. An unreadable ledger is a reason to withhold a figure; it is not a reason
to make it impossible to create a bot.

The handling: catch **only** that specific error class (anything else is a bug in
this layer and still escapes), return `null` for that account's capital block,
and **name the affected account on screen** rather than silently rendering fewer
tiles. The response therefore has **three distinct states, not two**, and the
distinction is load-bearing:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| a populated `assets` array | real headroom, per asset |
| `{ rowsRead: 0, assets: [] }` | an **unseeded** account — a real, common state, since `ledger.ts` creates no row automatically |
| `null` | the **read failed** — nothing is known |

Collapsing the last two would tell a human "this account can fund nothing" about
a read that never happened. That is section 5.6's rule, and it is the exact
distinction `readAccountCapital` already refuses to blur.

**2. `CapitalOnlyStrip`, for a funded asset with no bots.** The existing stat row
is keyed by **which capital assets have bots** (`accountTotals` folds the bot
list). A freshly funded account with nothing running therefore produces **no
strip to attach an AVAILABLE tile to** — and that is precisely the situation
where "what can I start a bot with" is the only interesting figure on the page.
Keying the tiles solely off the bot list would have hidden the figure in the one
case it exists for.

So a strip carrying **only** the AVAILABLE tiles was added for that case,
deliberately with no zeroed bot-derived figures beside it: there is no fleet
there to have allocated, held or earned anything, and "0.00 realized" would be an
assertion about trading that never happened.

**This is the one place the build went beyond a literal reading of the design,
and it is flagged as reversible.** It does not alter ALLOCATED, which still
appears if and only if bot totals exist for that asset.

A consequence worth recording: `AccountSummary`'s multi-asset note had to be
reworded, because the old text — "Bots span N capital assets" — is false for a
strip that has no bots. It now reads: "Totals are grouped per capital asset and
never summed across them. Available capital is per account and asset, and is
never summed across accounts either."

## PART 5 — A REAL, STRUCTURAL TESTING LIMIT

**This repository has no jsdom and no testing-library.** A test importing a
`.tsx` **collects zero tests rather than failing** inside the Workers pool this
suite runs in. Decision logs **44, 45, 46 and 48** already establish this; this
step did not discover it and did not change it.

The response was to push **every assertable decision out of the component and
into plain logic**: `dashboard/src/availableCapital.ts` decides how many cards
exist, which account and asset each belongs to, what its label says, which of the
two hint sentences it carries, whether it is flagged over-allocated, and the
order they appear in. All of it is covered by **23 tests**. The `.tsx` only
places what that module produced and chooses a colour.

**What remains genuinely unverified by any automated test in this repository:**

- **whether the amber CSS class actually reaches the rendered screen.** The tests
  assert `card.overAllocated === true`, which is a **flag on a data structure** —
  not a pixel, not a class attribute, not a computed style.
- **the tile's exact position in the grid**, including that it sits immediately
  beside ALLOCATED.

**This gap is not new and is not specific to this feature** — every other tile in
that row already carries it. But nothing in this entry should be read as claiming
these tests proved the visual rendering. They did not, and they cannot.

## PART 8 — NOT DONE / NOT VERIFIED

**The visual rendering gap from Part 5 remains true even after the live check.**
The operator confirmed the **number** and the **subtext** rendered correctly, and
that is real, meaningful evidence — it is more than any test in this repository
can produce. But it is **manual confirmation by a human looking at a screen, and
it is not a substitute for the missing test infrastructure.** No automated test
in this repo can assert it, nothing will catch a regression in it, and the amber
class in particular was **not** part of what was observed live — the live
confirmation covers the healthy case only.

**`CapitalOnlyStrip` (Part 4, item 2) has not been exercised live.** The
operator's current fleet has bots on every funded asset, so the funded-but-botless
path never rendered. It remains correct per its unit tests
(`assetsWithoutTotals`, three cases) and **has not been observed on a real
funded-but-botless asset.**

**ALLOCATED and the ledger's `total_allocated` remain two independently computed
figures that can drift.** ALLOCATED is the dashboard's own sum over polled bot
rows; AVAILABLE's subtrahend is `capital_ledger.total_allocated`. Their agreeing
is reconciliation's `ledger_allocation_drift` check, which exists precisely
because they can diverge. **This step does not reconcile them and was not asked
to** — a comment beside the two tiles says so in the source, so a future reader
does not mistake the pairing for a claim that they must match.

**The two caveats in Part 2 are documented, not mitigated.** Nothing detects an
unreconciled manual adjustment or deployed inventory and adjusts the figure; the
subtext's wording is the entire mitigation.
