## PART 1 — THE BUG, AND WHY IT IS WIDER THAN WHAT TRIGGERED IT

The alert that opened this named a partial fill followed by a cancellation:
`bot-qgo39d` held `0.00001000` base with `0.00000000` covered by resting sells,
after a buy at `75285.71` for `0.00015939` partially filled `0.00001` and was
then cancelled.

**That is the RARER of the real leak routes.** The investigation read the source
and found the dominant route needs no cancellation, no halt, no slot collision
and no repair:

> An **ordinary, successful** grid buy that fills in **more than one execution**
> places its replacement sell sized from **only the LAST execution's quantity**.

`fullyFilled` is `filledQuantity === order.quantity` — true on an order's *last*
execution, not only on its *only* one. So a buy filling `0.0004 + 0.0006` ran the
replacement branch once, holding `0.0006`, and rested a sell for `0.0006` against
a position of `0.0010`. The first slice stayed held with nothing selling it,
**permanently**: nothing re-runs replace-on-fill for a rung already missed.

**[OPERATOR]** Confirmed live by direct D1 query: two **other** real bots —
`bot-4xcq8p` and `bot-3trlgb` — were already holding uncovered base with zero
covering sell at the time of this investigation. **`bot-3trlgb`'s case came from
an ordinary, fully-filled, multi-execution buy with no halt or cancellation
involved.** That is the common path firing on a live bot, not an edge case
reasoned about in the abstract.

### The detection gap that let both hide

`uncoveredInventoryFindings` returned early on **any** non-`running` bot:

```ts
if (snapshot.state.status !== "running") return [];
```

**[OPERATOR]** Both affected bots had already halted for an unrelated reason
(`breakout_take_profit`) before anyone could see the uncovered inventory
underneath — and **the existing alert query for this condition returned ZERO rows
for both bots** at the time of discovery. Stated explicitly because it is the
proof that matters: **the detector could not see this on its own.** The
condition was real, present, and invisible to the one check written to find it.

### Five routes, not two

The investigation found at least five ways to reach uncovered inventory: the
`claimSlot` slot collision; `applyMissedFills` (by design); the multi-execution
sizing bug above; partial-fill-then-cancel; and a replacement whose placement
failed and was silently dropped (Part 3). The code comment at the detector
claimed **"TWO WAYS TO REACH IT"** and named the first two.

## PART 2 — STAGE A: THE ROOT CAUSE

`planFill` (`strategies/grid.ts`) sized a completing order's replacement sell
from the single triggering execution's `fillQuantity`, rather than from the
order's full cumulative filled quantity.

### The cost basis had to move with the quantity, or this would have been a new bug

Sizing correctly by quantity while carrying the **wrong** cost basis would have
traded one defect for another. The replacement's `costBasis` is what the eventual
round trip's realized profit is measured against; a sell covering `0.0010` while
carrying only the final slice's price would book the earlier slices' profit
against a price they were never bought at. So the fix computes a genuine
**quantity-weighted average** across every execution that contributed:
`sum(price × quantity) / sum(quantity)`, half-even at both steps.

### The data already existed — it simply was not read where the full picture was needed

**Confirmed before building anything:** `TrackedOrder.fills: readonly Fill[]`
(`shared/order-state.ts`) already retains **every** execution in application
order, and each `Fill` already carries its own `price` and `quantity`.
`applyFill` returns `effect.order` with the triggering execution already
appended. **Nothing had to be invented, reconstructed, or inferred.** The history
was sitting on the order record; the one place that needed all of it was reading
one element of it.

This was checked and stated **before** the fix was written, precisely because a
fabricated cost basis would have been worse than the bug being fixed.

### The deliberate refusal

When a fill completes an order but is **smaller than the order's own quantity**,
earlier executions must exist by construction — that is the only way to arrive
there. If the history is somehow unavailable, `acquisitionFor` **refuses** with a
`GridError` rather than guessing, because **both available guesses are wrong in
opposite directions**:

- Size from the **execution alone** → **under-covers** the position. This is
  precisely the original bug, reintroduced.
- Size from the **order's requested quantity** → **over-covers** it. On a
  partial-then-cancelled buy that rests a sell for base the bot never bought.

There is no honest third answer, so it declines to produce one. This matches the
discipline already established across tonight's fixes: `#recordCancellation`
refusing to synthesise a fill id, `#foldTerminalState` refusing to close an order
over a fill it never recorded, `repairPosition` refusing a grid bot at gate 1.
**Refuse rather than guess**, every time the alternative is a plausible number
with nothing behind it.

The single-execution path returns exactly the pair the old code used, which is
what makes the change additive rather than a rewrite of the common case.

## PART 3 — STAGE B: QUEUE INSTEAD OF DROP, AND TWO THINGS BUILT BEYOND THE BRIEF

### The bug, precisely

When a replacement could not be placed, **exactly one** outcome — `slot_occupied`
— was queued into `pendingReplacements` for retry. Every other outcome was
**silently dropped**: `skipped` (not constructible, typically dust under the
venue minimum), `throttled` (rate limited), `unresolved` (transport failure),
`recover` (the idempotency guard wanting the previous attempt resolved), and
`aborted` (the bot leaving `running` mid-flight). In all five the method still
returned `action: "replaced-sell"` to its caller — **reporting success while
nothing was resting on the exchange.**

Fixed: every non-placement is queued, and success is reported only when an order
is actually resting. The decision now runs through one exported predicate,
`gridOrderWasPlaced`, tied to the single site that mints the success action. The
old code told placed from not-placed by **listing failures**, which fails **open**
— every outcome nobody remembered to list read as success, which is exactly how
five of them were lost. Recognising the one success shape fails **closed**.

### Unplanned addition 1 — the existing drain silently undid the fix

`#drainReplacements` removed a queued intent after **exactly one** retry
attempt, on **any** outcome other than `slot_occupied`, with the stated reasoning
*"the intent has had its turn and must not be replayed."*

This is a **genuine, independently-discovered flaw in the existing code, not an
enhancement.** Stage B built as originally specified would have been **silently
undone one poll cycle later**: an intent queued at the fold site would be retried
once by the next drain and then discarded. The queue would have *looked* like a
real safety net — it is named one, it is alerted on as one — while still throwing
intents away after a single attempt. Fixing one door and leaving the other open
fixes neither.

The drain now removes an intent only once an order is actually resting. Replay is
safe: `#placeGridOrder`'s pre-send check refuses an occupied level **before**
anything is sent, and the idempotency guard answers `recover` rather than sending
a second order when a previous attempt is unresolved — so a retried intent cannot
become a duplicate resting order.

### Unplanned addition 2 — retention would have reproduced entry 18's alert flood

With retention, a replacement that is unconstructible for a **lasting** reason
(dust below the venue minimum) is re-attempted on every drain — and the drain runs
at the top of every poll pass and after every fold. `POLL_INTERVAL_MS` is
`30_000`, so that is **on the order of 2,880 alert rows per day for a single
stuck intent**, `order_not_constructible` being an unconditional insert.

That is **entry 18's already-known alert-volume problem**, repeated. Entry 18
recorded three findings writing **186 identical unresolved criticals in four
hours** and introduced standing alerts for exactly this shape; the same figure of
~2,880/day per bot is already written into `bot-instance.ts` twice as the reason
other conditions use standing alerts rather than plain inserts. Building Stage B
without addressing this would have knowingly recreated a problem this project had
already measured, named and solved.

**The fix:** `#placeGridOrder` gained `alertOnUnconstructible`, false **for the
drain only**. The first attempt still writes its row — a discrete event, reported
once. The **ongoing condition** is carried by `grid_replacement_queued`, an
existing **standing** (deduplicated) alert that states itself once and stays open
while the queue is non-empty, and whose message already says base is held with
nothing resting against it. The event and the condition are each reported by the
mechanism suited to them.

A reason that never clears therefore stays queued and stays alerted forever. That
is **intended rather than tolerated**: it is the honest end state for inventory
the ladder cannot cover by itself, and it is what Stage D will act on.

## PART 4 — STAGE C: WIDENING DETECTION, AND AN HONEST DOCUMENTATION CHANGE

The `status !== "running"` early return is removed. Halted grid bots holding
uncovered inventory are now detected and alerted.

Two things made this a one-line change rather than a larger one, both checked
from source: `RECONCILED_STATUSES` is `["created", "running", "halted"]`, so
halted bots were **already loaded and snapshotted** — only this function was
discarding them; and `stopped` is excluded upstream, so no snapshot of one ever
reaches here. The `exitOrderId` gate stays: a live liquidation sell **is** the
cover this looks for.

### DETECTION ONLY — confirmed, not assumed

Section 9's standing rule is unchanged: reconciliation halts and alerts and never
auto-corrects. The meaningful tier's action is `haltBot`, and **`halt()` is
idempotent** — an already-halted bot returns `already_halted` and changes
nothing, as the worker's own port comment states. So a halted bot found here
gains an **alert and nothing else**. Nothing is cancelled, nothing is placed.
This is asserted against the exchange itself in a test (Part 6), not merely
reasoned about, so a future "helpful" corrective call fails loudly.

### The comment correction, and why no new count replaced the old one

The docblock claimed **"TWO WAYS TO REACH IT"** and named the slot collision and
`applyMissedFills`. The investigation found at least five real routes, one of
which — the multi-execution fill — was not an edge case at all but the ordinary
path.

The obvious fix is to write "FIVE WAYS". **That was rejected**, because it repeats
the original mistake at a larger number: an exhaustive list here was wrong within
weeks of being written and read as authoritative the whole time it was wrong. The
comment now states no count and instead records that the detector is deliberately
**CAUSE-BLIND** — it asks one question of the ladder's own current shape (is more
base held than resting sells cover), and that question stays correct however the
gap arrived, **including by routes nobody has thought of yet**. `applyMissedFills`
is still named, because it remains the one route that is *by design*.

## PART 5 — THE ONE TEST REMOVED, AND WHY REMOVING IT WAS CORRECT

Removed: `"stays silent on a halted bot, whose ladder was swept on purpose"`, with
the comment *"A human already holds this one, and the halt cleared the slots
itself."*

**Both premises are false.**

1. **A halt cancels resting orders; it never sells inventory the bot already
   holds.** `#cancelOpenOrders` cancels and reconciles open orders. Uncovered base
   survives a halt exactly as it was — the sweep does not touch `heldQuantity`.
2. **"A human already holds it" presupposed a notification this system never
   sent.** The human only "already knows" if something told them, and **this
   detector was the only thing that would have.** Skipping halted bots is
   precisely why two of them sat uncovered and unreported.

**[OPERATOR]** The live evidence is the second premise failing in practice: both
affected bots were halted on `breakout_take_profit` and the alert query returned
zero rows for both.

So this was **a wrong assumption pinned in test form**, and a passing test was
actively holding the detector shut on the exact population that most needed it.
Removing it is a correction, not a convenience — and it is the only existing test
this session removed. Nothing else in the suite was edited; every other change is
an addition.

### Mutation testing, per stage

- **Stage A — 9/9 killed.** Including `quantity += → =` and `notional += → =`,
  weighted price → last fill price, basis → single fill price, refusal gate
  disabled, half-even → floor, empty-history guard removed, and the call site's
  `effect.order.fills → order.fills`. **The reintroduced-original-bug mutant is
  named explicitly**: replacement quantity `acquired.quantity → fillQuantity`,
  which is the bug exactly as it shipped — **killed by 3 tests**.
- **Stage B — 6/6 killed.** Predicate reverted to `!== "slot_occupied"`;
  predicate always true; fold site reverted to slot-occupied-only; drain reverted
  to remove-on-any-outcome. **The alert-flood-reintroduction mutant is named
  explicitly**: the drain passing `alertOnUnconstructible = true`, restoring an
  alert on every retry — killed.
- **Stage C — 5 killed, 1 EQUIVALENT MUTANT.** Killed: status gate restored; gate
  flipped to halted-only; `exitOrderId` gate removed; cover comparison
  `>= → >`; and sell-only cover → all-slots cover.

  Two survived the first pass. **`sell-only cover → all slots` was a genuine
  coverage gap** — no existing test had a resting *buy* alongside uncovered
  inventory, which is the most common live ladder shape, and summing every slot
  would have read those buys as cover and reported the ladder healthy. A test was
  added and the mutant now dies.

  **`heldQuantity <= ZERO` guard removed is an EQUIVALENT MUTANT, not an accepted
  coverage gap.** No input can distinguish it, for a concrete mathematical
  reason: `restingSell` is a `reduce` starting at `ZERO` and adding
  `slot.quantity` values, each of which is a placed order's quantity and
  therefore strictly positive — so `restingSell >= 0` always holds. The very next
  line is `if (restingSell >= ladder.heldQuantity) return []`. Whenever
  `heldQuantity <= 0`, that comparison is necessarily true and the function
  returns `[]` regardless of whether the removed guard ran. The guard is purely
  defensive and its removal changes **no observable output for any input**. No
  test was contrived to "kill" it, because none can.

## PART 7 — TWO REFINEMENTS TO THE INVESTIGATION'S OWN FINDINGS

Recorded because a build that quietly improves on its own investigation teaches
nothing about how good the investigation was.

1. **The investigation was too generous about the drain.** It stated the existing
   queue *"already retries them on every poll and after every fold"*, offered as
   the reason queuing was a cheap and adequate remedy. Part 3 found that it
   retried **exactly once** and then dropped the intent on any outcome other than
   `slot_occupied`. **The queue was never the safety net it appeared to be from
   reading the code alone** — and that misreading came from reading the drain's
   loop and its stated intent rather than its removal condition.
2. **The alert-flood risk was not predicted.** It does not appear anywhere in the
   investigation's six enumerated fix options. It was found **during the build**,
   as a direct consequence of the retention fix, not in advance.
