## PART 1 — THE ACTUAL ROOT CAUSE, FOUND BY TRACING RATHER THAN ASSUMED

**The defect is not at site 2868, 4707, or 4856 individually. It is in
`#cancelOpenOrders`.**

When `#recordCancellation` resolves an order during a halt sweep — the exchange
confirmed the cancellation and the local record was closed — **nothing clears
that order's ladder rung.** The sweep never touched the ladder at all, and the
only function that clears a single slot, `#foldTerminalState`, is reached solely
from the poll, which reads `openOrderIds` and therefore **never looks at an
order the sweep just removed from it.**

So a manually halted grid bot sat indefinitely holding rungs naming orders that
were cancelled, resolved and gone.

**ALL THREE READING SITES WERE INDEPENDENTLY REPRODUCING SYMPTOMS OF THAT ONE
MISSING STEP.** Each of them built `openOrderIds` by wholesale-assigning from
the ladder's slots, so **any** of them would put a stale, already-resolved id
back on the tracked list whenever it ran. Three symptoms, one cause.

### The correction to entry 74 — a narrowing, not an overturning

Entry 74 identified the stale-ladder mechanism reaching site **4707**, and
recorded in its Part 5 that a future fix "needs to account for both sites
together."

**This session found the IDENTICAL mechanism also reaches site 4856** — entry
74's *own already-shipped fix*, at the fold. Both sites' results include
`ladderOpenOrderIds(...)` wholesale, so both resurrect a stale rung. **Entry 74
did not state this.**

Its instinct was right and its enumeration was short by one. **Fixing only site
2868, as literally deferred, would have left both 4856 and 4707 still
vulnerable to the same resurrection.**

### Why the root was findable at all

`closeOrder` has **exactly two call sites** in this object:
`#foldTerminalState`, which has always cleared the rung, and
`#recordCancellation`, which never did. **One hole, countable, not inferred** —
which is what made a root fix a defensible choice rather than a hopeful one.

## PART 2 — WHY THE ROOT FIX, AND WHY IT IS SAFER THAN THREE SYMPTOM PATCHES

Rather than apply entry 74's `resolved`/`kept`/`created` discipline
independently at each of the three reading sites — three copies of one guard,
three places to drift, and a fourth needed the moment a fourth reader is
written — **this closes the single point where the invariant actually breaks.**

**The change:** `#cancelOpenOrders`' resolved-set mutate now also nulls the
ladder rung of every id it resolved.

**It uses the SAME `resolved` set that already governs the list**, not a second,
independently-formed opinion about what counts as resolved. That set is
precisely the ids `#recordCancellation` returned `true` for, plus the ones the
loop skipped as already terminal.

### Why this cannot reintroduce entry 57/64's original problem

Entries **57** and **64** made one determination safe: *when is an order
genuinely resolved?* Their answer is `#recordCancellation`'s return value, and
everything in `stillOpen` — the unconfirmed cancellation whose order may still
be live, and the record the gate refused to close over a fill it had not
recorded — is **not** resolved.

**The slot clearing is downstream of, and gated by, that exact determination.**
It forms no opinion of its own; it consumes the one already made. Everything
retained keeps **both** its id on the list **and** its rung on the ladder.

**The slot follows the resolution. It never leads it, and it never duplicates
the judgement.** That is the same anti-drift reasoning entries 57 and 61 used to
refuse a second, independently-worded comparison of the same two quantities.

### The practical result

The invariant **"a non-null ladder rung names an order this bot believes is
genuinely live"** becomes **PROVABLE rather than assumed** — provable because
the two `closeOrder` call sites are countable and both now clear.

**All three reading sites (2868, 4707, 4856) become correct as a consequence,
and entry 74's already-shipped code at 4707 and 4856 was not edited at all.**

## PART 4 — A REAL BUG FOUND AS A SIDE EFFECT, NOT PART OF THE BRIEF

**Entry 70's uncovered-inventory detector was reading the same stale data, and
failing in the direction that matters.**

`uncoveredInventoryFindings` (`src/reconciliation/reconcile.ts:615`) sums
**sell-side ladder rungs as protective coverage**:

```ts
const restingSell = ladder.slots.reduce(
  (total, slot) => (slot !== null && slot.side === "sell" ? total + slot.quantity : total),
  ZERO,
);
```

Before this fix, **a stale rung left behind by a CANCELLED sell on a manually
halted bot counted as coverage that did not exist.** The detector would compute
`restingSell` from orders that were not resting at all, find `restingSell >=
heldQuantity`, and return no finding.

**A FALSE NEGATIVE in the one detector built specifically to catch uncovered
inventory.**

### This does NOT affect entries 70 or 71's conclusions

**`bot-4xcq8p` and `bot-3trlgb` both went through `#gridExit`**, which nulls
every slot unconditionally. With every rung null, `restingSell` is `ZERO` and
`uncovered = heldQuantity` exactly — which is precisely what entry 71 Part 1
reasoned and what the operator's live figures bore out. **Their detector reads
were unaffected by this blind spot, and nothing in entries 70 or 71 requires
revision.**

**It was a real gap on a DIFFERENT population**: manually halted bots
specifically, which are the ones whose rungs are never cleared. It existed until
this fix, and **no prior test asserted the old, wrong behaviour** — the full
suite passed unchanged, so nothing had pinned the false negative in place.

### Five tests, named

1. **`the halt sweep clears the rungs of the orders it resolved`** — the root
   fix asserted directly: after a sweep that retains one order and resolves the
   rest, the ladder's rungs equal **exactly `[retained]`**. What the sweep could

4. **`UNREGRESSED: a clean halt leaves no tracked ids and no rungs`** — ids and
   rungs go together on the ordinary path.
