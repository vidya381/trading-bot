## PART 1 — THE ROOT CAUSE THIS CLOSES

`DcaPosition` had `applyEntry` and **no inverse**. Within a cycle `quantity` and
`cost` only ever grew, and both were zeroed together, wholesale, at
`#completeCycle` / `#completeLiquidation` — reached only when an exit sell FULLY
fills.

`#applyFillToOrder`'s mutate was, and had always been, `if (!isExit) { applyEntry(...) }`
with **no `else` at all**. On an exit fill the only state it touched was
`openOrderIds`, and only when `effect.fullyFilled`. So an exit that filled
PARTIALLY — whether still resting or later cancelled — **left the position
completely untouched**, and the bot went on counting coin it had already sold.

That is how `bot-b23y63` came to believe it held 3.7× what it actually did, with
its stop-loss and take-profit both computed from the inflated figure. Read fresh
this session before anything was written: the branch was **unchanged by fixes
1–3**, so nothing about that description had gone stale.

## PART 2 — THE INVESTIGATION, INCLUDING TWO REAL DEVIATIONS FROM THE BRIEF

### Q2 — the same cost model as entry 59, with one deliberate refinement

Same **proportional** model, because a normally-run position and a repaired one
must not be able to disagree. One difference, and it is a refinement rather than
an inconsistency:

- **Entry 59's repair measured the sold leg as a PRODUCT:** `soldQuantity ×
  averageEntryPrice`. Correct there — it runs **once**, over a settled total.
- **`applyExit` measures it as a REMAINDER:** `cost − newCost`.

The two agree to within rounding on any single exit. But this one runs on **every
fill**, and only the remainder form is **self-balancing**: the amounts removed
across every fill of a full exit sum to exactly the cycle's original cost, at any
number of partials, so `cost` reaches **exactly** `ZERO` when the position goes
flat rather than approximately zero. The product form would leave a rounding
residue per fill, accumulating with the number of partials — which is precisely
the regime this function lives in and the repair does not.

### Q3 — realized profit now books INCREMENTALLY, and that is a visible change

Booked on every exit fill, as it lands. **The reasoning is that the alternative is
incoherent**, not merely less tidy: `applyExit` removes the cost basis backing
that quantity **in the same step**, so a profit figure deferred to cycle
completion would have **nothing left to measure against** by the time it ran. You
cannot decrement cost incrementally and book profit wholesale.

It also gives the partial-then-cancelled case a home for the first time — that
leg's realized PnL previously existed nowhere at all until a manual repair went
looking for it.

**Recorded as a real, user-visible behaviour change:** `realizedGross` now MOVES
DURING a cycle rather than jumping once at its end. Anything reading that number
— the dashboard, the account rollup — will see it change mid-cycle where it
previously would not have.

### Q4 — THE DEVIATION, and it was a catastrophic double-count caught before it shipped

**The brief assumed `#completeCycle` could stay mostly as-is** once `position` was
already near-zero. **Investigation found that FALSE, and the consequence was not
minor.**

The old formula was `gross = mul(exit.filledQuantity, exit.price) − state.position.cost`.
With `applyExit` running first, `position.cost` is **already ZERO** by the time
completion runs — so `gross` would evaluate to the **ENTIRE exit's proceeds**, and
that whole amount would be added to `realizedGross` a **second time**, on top of
what had already been booked correctly, fill by fill. Every completed cycle would
have roughly doubled its reported profit.

So both `#completeCycle` and `#completeLiquidation` became **report-only
cleanup**. They no longer add to `realizedGross` at all; they derive the figure
for the audit row and the alert from the exit's own fills against `entries[]`'
costs — `entries` is still intact at that moment, and its costs sum to exactly
what the cycle spent. `EMPTY_POSITION` still runs, now clearing `entries`,
`additionalBuysUsed` and `lastEntryPrice` rather than performing any decrement or
PnL arithmetic.

Recorded as a **self-caught catastrophic-double-count risk**, not a small
adjustment. `#completeCycle` and `#completeLiquidation` are original code rather
than fixes 1–3's, so changing them stayed inside the brief's stated boundary.

### Q5 — no symmetrical exit-side concept is needed, checked rather than assumed

`entries[]` is buy-side **by definition** — one element per applied BUY fill —
and every reader was checked directly: `decide` uses `entries.length` in a
stop-loss message, `#completeCycle` audits the count, `DcaPositionView.tsx`
renders the rows. Nothing reads it expecting sells. `additionalBuysUsed` and
`lastEntryPrice` are likewise entry-side: selling does not hand back a buy
allowance, and it does not move the price the next drop trigger is measured from.
`applyExit` appends nothing and removes nothing from any of the three.

### The dust leak — CONFIRMED orthogonal

Entry 59's finding stands and is untouched. It arises at **sell sizing**:
`#placeTakeProfitSell` rounds the exit quantity DOWN onto the symbol's step, and
`#completeCycle` zeroes the position anyway. `applyExit` only ever sees
quantities that were **actually filled**; it changes neither the rounding nor the
wholesale zero-out. Not addressed here, as before.

## PART 3 — WHAT WAS BUILT

- **`DcaExit`, `DcaExitEffect` and `applyExit` in `dca.ts`** — the missing inverse
  of `applyEntry`. Refuses a non-positive quantity and refuses an exit larger than
  the position (unreachable in ordinary operation, since an exit is sized from
  `position.quantity` and rounded down and `applyFill` caps fills at the order's
  quantity — so reaching it means the books already disagree with themselves, and
  silently clamping would hide the very condition this exists to stop producing).
- **Wired into `#applyFillToOrder`'s exit branch**, so it runs on **every** exit
  fill rather than being deferred to completion. `effect.quoteDelta` is positive
  on a sell and is already the notional at the price the fill ACTUALLY executed
  at, so the proceeds figure is taken from there and nothing is re-derived.
- **`#completeCycle` and `#completeLiquidation` converted to report-only cleanup**,
  per Q4.
- **`exitOrderId` / `exitKind` clearing is UNCHANGED** — still only on full
  completion or repair, exactly as specified, and confirmed not to need
  modification. This fix changes what `position` says, not when the exit pointer
  clears.

## PART 4 — THE HEADLINE RESULT: TWO ENTRY-59 TESTS BROKE, AND THAT IS THE PROOF

Two tests from entry 59 **failed on the first run** after this fix:

```
× computes bot-b23y63's residual    AssertionError: expected 43085n to be 158519n
× computes bot-bl4e7c's residual    AssertionError: expected '2.40469200' to be '1.99999200'
```

**Those failures are the fix working, and they deserve to be the headline rather
than a footnote.**

Both tests built the damaged position shape by running the **ordinary code path**
and then asserted the damage as the starting point the repair tool would fix. The
first expected `position.quantity` to still be the full `0.00158519` after a
partial exit; the second expected `realizedGross` to still be cycle 1's
`1.99999200`, with cycle 2's partial exit having realized nothing.

**After this fix, that damaged shape can no longer be produced by the ordinary
path.** The position is already correct by the time the cancellation happens, and
the partial exit's profit is already booked. The setup helpers still run
faithfully; what they produce is a healthy bot.

Both tests were updated, with comments recording why, to assert the **composed**
behaviour — and they now prove something stronger than they did before:

- the repair tool finds `before.quantity === after.quantity === "0.00043085"`
  and `"0.00092962"` respectively — **nothing left to repair on the quantities**;
- it still reports `would_repair`, because the **stale exit pointer** is still
  there, which is fix 5's explicitly unchanged behaviour;
- and `realizedGross` is **NOT booked a second time** — the repair's `reducing`
  hinge is false, so entry 59's idempotency guard holds across the two fixes.

**This is the clean demonstration that fixes 1, 3 and 5 agree with one another
rather than silently conflicting.** Three independently-built mechanisms — a
cancellation gate, a repair tool, and an incremental decrement — meeting on the
same state and producing one consistent answer.

**Entry 59's repair tool is unchanged and remains correct for bots already
damaged under the OLD code**, which is exactly how `bot-b23y63` and `bot-bl4e7c`
reached that state. What changed is that nothing will produce a new one.
