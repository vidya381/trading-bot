## PART 1 — THE BUG, AND HOW IT SURFACED

### What fired

Two `price_feed_value_frozen` alerts, both `critical`, both still unresolved
when the session began:

| alert | source | frozen value | raised |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `3b3e0aa4…` | `price-feed:kraken:BTCUSDT` | `77223.56000000` | 16:33:58Z |
| `bf7526ac…` | `price-feed:kraken:ETHUSDT` | `2433.42000000` | 15:02:02Z |

Three real bots were `running` on those pairs with real capital: `bot-hl8z62`
(trailing_stop, BTCUSDT), `bot-vnjo24` (grid, BTCUSDT), `bot-qfhkqo` (dca,
ETHUSDT), all on account `kraken-main`.

**A note on the timestamps, because it cost a wrong first hypothesis.** The
alerts were reported to this session as having started at 11:23Z and 09:52Z.
They had not: the D1 `created_at` values are 16:33:58Z and 15:02:02Z, exactly
**five hours later**, and the freeze onsets (ten candles earlier) are 16:23Z and
14:52Z. The reported times were a UTC−5 rendering labelled UTC. Every
cross-venue comparison in this entry uses the D1 values. **The five-hour
display discrepancy is a real, separate, unfixed finding** and is listed in
Part 7.

### The first question, and the answer that reframed everything

The question asked first was whether Kraken itself was flat — a thin market
genuinely not trading would make the detector a false positive, and Kraken's
XBT/USDT is thin enough for that to be plausible (zero-volume minutes are
routine on it). It was not flat. At the freeze onsets, Kraken's real market was
moving and, at 16:32–16:35, actively traded (35, 23, 19, 17 trades per minute):

| | frozen feed value | Kraken real, at onset | Kraken 24h volume |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BTCUSDT | 77223.56 | 76999.4 → 76930 (moving) | 83.19 BTC / 3013 trades |
| ETHUSDT | 2433.42 | 2440.30 → 2442.98 (moving) | 2481 ETH / 3407 trades |

**Neither frozen value appears anywhere in Kraken's 12 hours of 1-minute
history.** That killed the false-positive hypothesis and replaced it with a
worse one: the number was real, plausible, and from somewhere else.

### Where the number was from

Gemini's public ticker, queried directly:

```
GET https://api.gemini.com/v1/pubticker/btcusdt  ->  "last":"77223.56000"
GET https://api.gemini.com/v1/pubticker/ethusdt  ->  "last":"2433.42000"
```

Exact matches for both frozen values. Confirmed further by Gemini's own trade
history, which contains **2459.15** and **2476.78** — the two *earlier*
`kraken:ETHUSDT` frozen values from the same day's alert history. Five distinct
frozen values, five Gemini prints.

### Root cause

`durable-objects/price-feed.ts` built its codec in a **field initialiser**:

```ts
#deps: PriceFeedDependencies = {
  …
  codec: new GeminiPriceFeedCodec(),
  …
};
```

`GeminiPriceFeedCodec` was the only implementation of `PriceFeedCodec` in the
codebase; there was no Kraken one. `setDependencies`/`attach` overrode it from
tests only. The `exchange` half of the object's own `{exchange, pair}` config —
the thing its Durable Object name is derived from — was read in exactly two
places, `#configLabel()` and `#alertSource()`, **both of which produce strings
for humans**. It never selected a URL, a subscribe frame, or a parser.

So every `kraken:*` feed opened `wss://api.gemini.com/v2/marketdata`, subscribed
to Gemini's book, and forwarded Gemini's prices to bots placing real orders on
Kraken through `kraken/client.ts`. **Since creation.** Not a regression, not a
race, not an outage — the wrong answer was the only answer the code could give,
and every layer downstream was working perfectly on it.

### Why nothing saw it

This is the part worth keeping. The feed was **indistinguishable from healthy by
every check the system has**:

* the socket was connected and never dropped;
* heartbeats were current, so `price_feed_blind` stayed clear;
* `#forwardClosed` stamps every price with **receipt** time, so `lastPriceAt`
  was fresh by construction and `price_updates_stale` stayed clear;
* the book was uncrossed;
* the prices were real, live, correctly parsed prices of the right *asset*.

Entry [86](86.md) PART 6 predicted exactly this: all three spec-5.7 detectors
are SYMPTOM detectors, and "a feed that is simply WRONG while looking healthy
passes all three." What eventually broke the silence was not the wrongness. It
was that **Gemini's USDT books are nearly dead** — 2.36 BTC and 0.999 ETH of
24-hour volume against Kraken's 83.19 BTC — so the wrong venue was also a venue
that stops printing for hours, and `price_feed_value_frozen` fired on the
staleness. The feed had to break in a *second*, unrelated way before the first
fault became visible.

### Why the independent cross-check did not catch it

Entry [97](97.md) built `reconciliation/price-cross-check.ts` specifically to
catch "a feed that is simply WRONG while looking healthy," reading Kraken
directly as an independent reference. **It was deployed, it was running, and it
correctly stayed silent.**

`DIVERGENCE_THRESHOLD` is 2% (`price-cross-check.ts:113`). Measured live during
the incident:

| pair | feed (Gemini) | reference (Kraken) | divergence | threshold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BTCUSDT | 77223.56 | 77278.60 | **−0.071%** | 2% |
| ETHUSDT | 2433.42 | 2459.80 | **−1.072%** | 2% |

Both well inside. **This is not a threshold that was set too loosely — it is a
check aimed at the wrong failure.** It was built to catch an *implausible* price.
This bug delivered a real, live, entirely plausible price of the correct asset,
sourced from the wrong exchange; two venues quoting the same instrument agree to
well under 2% almost all the time, so the check had nothing to see. Tightening
it would trade a real detection for a stream of false alerts on ordinary
inter-venue spread, and would still miss this on a calm day.

**The threshold was deliberately NOT touched.** The correct fix is that codec
selection is right by construction, which is Part 4. Entry 97 closed the gap it
described; this entry closes a gap *upstream* of it, in the thing being checked.

### The exposure, and the decision to hold

All three bots were halted on the operator's instruction at 17:30:24Z,
17:30:35Z and 17:30:53Z via `POST /api/bots/:id/halt` — the real
`BotInstance.halt("manual", …)` path, not a D1 write. (A direct D1 `status`
update would have been a **fake halt**: the row is a mirror, and each Durable
Object would have kept its own `running` state and kept trading. That trap was
identified and avoided.) The halt cancelled `bot-vnjo24`'s resting 78,000 sell
**unfilled** at 17:30:36Z, leaving zero open orders on the account.

Marked to real Kraken prices at halt:

| bot | holding | cost | value | P/L |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bot-hl8z62` | 0.00025435 BTC | $19.91 | $19.68 | −$0.23 |
| `bot-vnjo24` | 0.00008441 BTC | $6.50 | $6.53 | +$0.03 |
| `bot-qfhkqo` | 0.00242250 ETH | $5.99 | $5.97 | −$0.03 |
| **total** | | **$32.41** | **$32.18** | **−$0.23** |

**~$32 total, near break-even.** A deliberate decision was made to **hold rather
than liquidate.** Halting removes the active risk — a bot acting on a wrong
price — while liquidating would crystallise a loss and incur three lots of fees
to avoid an exposure of thirty-two dollars. The positions are now unmanaged
spot with no automated stop, which is stated plainly as the accepted trade: at
this size that is a smaller risk than selling into a thin book to escape it.
`liquidate` remains available per bot and each is already halted, which that
endpoint requires.

**The defect is severe; the money at risk was not.** Both halves of that are
true and neither should be used to soften the other — the same code would have
run identically at any size.

## PART 2 — LIVE VERIFICATION: THREE ASSUMED FACTS, ALL WRONG

The Kraken build has now had assumptions fail at the catalogue layer (entry
[95](95.md)), the rate-limit layer ([96](96.md)/[98](98.md)), the order-ceiling
layer ([99](99.md)) and the resolver layer ([94](94.md)). The same discipline
was applied here before a line of the codec was written: **every fact below was
observed on `wss://ws.kraken.com/v2` with a real, credential-free subscription
on 2026-09-10, not read from documentation.** Three of them were wrong in ways
that would have shipped silently.

### (a) The v2 socket uses a different symbol format than REST — and rejects REST's

REST wants `XBTUSDT`. `/public/AssetPairs` reports this pair's `wsname` as
`XBT/USDT`, and `catalogue.ts:157` records the verified property that "every
live pair's wsname IS its altname once separators are stripped." Every one of
those facts is true, and **all of them are the wrong answer for v2.** Sent live:

```
{"method":"subscribe","params":{"channel":"ohlc","symbol":["XBT/USDT"],"interval":1}}
-> {"error":"Currency pair not supported XBT/USDT","success":false,…}

{"method":"subscribe","params":{"channel":"ohlc","symbol":["BTCUSDT"],"interval":1}}
-> {"error":"Currency pair not in ISO 4217-A3 format BTCUSDT","success":false,…}

{"method":"subscribe","params":{"channel":"ohlc","symbol":["btc/usdt"],"interval":1}}
-> {"error":"Currency pair not supported btc/usdt","success":false,…}

{"method":"subscribe","params":{"channel":"ohlc","symbol":["BTC/USDT"],"interval":1}}
-> {"success":true,"result":{"symbol":"BTC/USDT",…}}
```

v2 names assets in **ISO-4217-A3** form — `BTC`, never Kraken's historic `XBT` —
which is the convention this system *already stores*. So the translation is not
a venue lookup at all: split our own pair and insert a slash. Had this been
taken from `wsname`, as every other Kraken file in this build correctly does,
the feed would have subscribed to a symbol Kraken refuses and received nothing,
forever — see Part 3.

### (b) `timestamp` is deprecated; the venue says so in its own ack

Every successful `ohlc` subscription returns:

```json
"warnings":["timestamp is deprecated, use interval_begin"]
```

Each data row carries **both** fields, and they are one minute apart:

```json
{"interval_begin":"2026-09-10T17:32:00.000000000Z", "timestamp":"2026-09-10T17:33:00.000000Z"}
```

`interval_begin` is the open; the deprecated `timestamp` is the **exclusive
end**. Reading the obvious-looking `timestamp` as an open time would have
**shifted every candle in the system forward by exactly one minute** — a
consistent, plausible, entirely silent error that no assertion in the codebase
would catch, because every candle would still be well-formed, monotonic and
fresh. It would have shown up as the feed being a minute behind the market, and
would have been extremely hard to attribute.

`interval_begin` carries **nanosecond** precision (9 fractional digits).
`Date.parse` was verified to truncate it to milliseconds correctly
(`…T17:32:00.000000000Z` → `1789061520000`, exact) and to return `NaN` on
malformed input, which `candleTime` converts into a `malformed` event rather
than an `Invalid Date` propagating into a `Money`.

### (c) Heartbeat cadence is 4–5× Gemini's — so nothing needed changing

Kraken's heartbeat is the bare `{"channel":"heartbeat"}`, with **no timestamp
of its own** (receipt time is the only available clock; the `PriceFeedCodec`
interface already passes it). Measured live: **27 frames in 28 seconds, ≈1/s.**

`STALENESS_MS` is 20 000, commented as "~4 missed heartbeats of headroom" —
which implies Gemini's ≈5s cadence. For Kraken the same constant is **≈20
missed heartbeats of headroom**. Strictly safer.

**This is the fact that was checked in order to change something, and produced a
change of nothing.** The explicit question was whether Kraken's public socket
needs different connection or reconnection handling. It does not: no client-side
ping is required, no auth handshake exists for public `ohlc`, the `status` frame
on connect is informational, and the faster heartbeat only widens the existing
margin. Backoff, `BLIND_ESCALATION_MS`, `SLOW_RETRY_MS` and the health-check
alarm are untouched.

**A fourth fact was checked and held.** Rollover was measured across two live
minute boundaries: an `update` carries **only** the in-progress candle and never
re-sends the one that just closed (`carriesPrev=false`, 2/2). This is the same
CURRENT-ONLY shape the step 14 probe found on Gemini, so
`PriceFeed.#ingestCandles` — which closes a candle when a newer `openTime`
appears rather than trusting any `closed` flag — handles Kraken unchanged. The
reconnect `snapshot` (9 rows, oldest-first, quiet minutes simply absent) is
exactly the gap-backfill batch that method already expects.

## PART 3 — TWO FINDINGS BEYOND THE BUG REPORT

Neither of these was in the brief. Both were found while building the fix.

### (i) `AUSD` is a real currency, and it breaks the obvious split

Translating `BTCUSDT` → `BTC/USDT` needs the pair split into base and quote.
`research/concentration.ts:245` already solves this shape and documents the
ordering rule with care: **longest quote suffix first**, because with
`["USD","USDT"]` the wrong way round, `BTCUSDT` strips `USD` and yields `BTCT`.
That reasoning is correct and the comment is right.

Applied to Kraken's real catalogue, it is **wrong 42 times out of 1449**.

`AUSD` is a **genuine, distinct Kraken quote currency** — not a typo, not a
mis-parse of `USD`, a real asset with real pairs. Because it is longer than
`USD`, longest-first strips it preferentially, and **every base asset ending in
the letter A quoted in USD splits wrongly**:

```
ADAUSD    -> AD  + AUSD     (should be ADA  + USD)
GALAUSD   -> GAL + AUSD     (should be GALA + USD)
KAVAUSD   -> KAV + AUSD     (should be KAVA + USD)
ARPAUSD, BERAUSD, ENAUSD, GIGAUSD, ALPHAUSD … 42 in total
```

Every one of those would have produced a symbol Kraken rejects.

**The fix, and its honest limits.** Pair → (base, quote) is *genuinely
ambiguous* from a concatenated string; no pure rule is correct for all 1449, and
pretending otherwise would be the real error. So the quote table is ordered by
**live catalogue frequency** (`USD` has 666 pairs, `AUSD` has 2), which scores
1444/1449, and the five remaining pairs — whose base genuinely ends in a more
popular quote's text — are carried as a small, explicitly listed exception
table:

| pair | correct split | why frequency ordering fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ETHPYUSD` | `ETH/PYUSD` | `USD` matches first → `ETHPY/USD` |
| `BTCPYUSD` | `BTC/PYUSD` | as above |
| `MONAUSD` | `MON/AUSD` | `USD` matches first → `MONA/USD` |
| `BTCAUSD` | `BTC/AUSD` | as above |
| `XRPRLUSD` | `XRP/RLUSD` | as above |

Together: **1449/1449 exact**, verified by round-tripping the entire live
`AssetPairs` catalogue. Then **12 derived symbols were subscribed to on the live
v2 socket — including all 5 exceptions — and all 12 returned `success:true`.**

### (ii) The more important find: a rejected subscription had the *same shape* as the bug

While building (i), the failure mode of getting a symbol wrong was traced, and
it is this:

**Kraken answers a bad symbol with `{"success":false,"error":…}` and then sends
nothing. Forever. The socket stays open. Heartbeats keep arriving at 1/s.**

Which means `price_feed_blind` stays clear, `price_updates_stale` stays clear —
the exact same green-across-the-board silence as the Gemini misrouting, reached
through a completely different door. Under the previous handling, a
`method` reply was an unrecognised message and would have been `ignored`.

**This is now caught proactively.** `parseMessage` reads method replies and
converts `success:false` into a **`malformed`** event, which the Durable Object
raises as `price_feed_malformed` — **within about a second of connecting**,
since the ack is one of the first frames on the wire.

The distinction worth recording: the exception table in (i) is a snapshot of a
catalogue that changes, and it will eventually go stale. **It is not the last
line of defence.** (ii) is. Today's bug came through a hardcoded codec; the next
one of this shape could come through a Kraken listing change making a pair
un-subscribable, and it would now announce itself in one second instead of
never. **The class of failure is closed, not just the instance.**

## PART 4 — THE STRUCTURAL FIX

Two changes, and the second matters more than the first.

### The table

`exchange/price-feed-dispatch.ts` is a total
`Readonly<Record<ExchangeId, PriceFeedCodec | null>>`:

```ts
export const PRICE_FEED_CODECS: Readonly<Record<ExchangeId, PriceFeedCodec | null>> =
  Object.freeze({
    binance: null,
    gemini: new GeminiPriceFeedCodec(),
    kraken: new KrakenPriceFeedCodec(),
  });
```

This is deliberately the **same construction as `EXCHANGE_RESOLVERS`**
(`workers/exchange-dispatch.ts`), `METHOD_COSTS` and
`VENUE_PUBLISHES_INSTRUMENT_TYPE`, for the reasons entry [94](94.md) recorded:

* **Total**, so widening `ExchangeId` **fails to compile here** until the new
  venue is answered for. A hardcoded field has no such forcing function — which
  is the whole story of this entry.
* **`null` rather than a missing key**, so "does this build have a feed for this
  venue" is a question code can *ask*, the property `venue-wiring.ts` was built
  around.
* **`null` throws, naming the venue.** `priceFeedCodecFor("binance")` raises
  rather than returning something that works. **A codec that works is precisely
  the wrong answer here** — that is what Gemini's was.

`binance: null` is the honest entry: Binance has a REST client and no
market-data codec. Under the old code a Binance feed would have silently dialled
Gemini too.

### The resolution point, which is the actual root cause

The table alone would not have prevented this. The deeper fault was **when** the
codec was chosen: a field initialiser runs at construction, and a `PriceFeed`
does not know its own `{exchange, pair}` at construction — `configure()` supplies
it when the first subscriber arrives. **A codec chosen before the config exists
cannot depend on it, so hardcoding one was the only thing that could compile.**
That ordering is the reason the bug existed, and it is the thing that changed:

```ts
#codec(config: PriceFeedConfig): PriceFeedCodec {
  return this.#deps.codec ?? priceFeedCodecFor(config.exchange);
}
```

Resolved per use — at parse, at connect, at subscribe — from the object's own
config. The `codec` dependency survives as a **tests-only override**, documented
as carrying the original bug's shape and `null` on every deployed object.

Codec resolution and `socketUrl` were also moved **inside** the connect `try`,
so an unservable venue becomes an ordinary failed open — backoff,
`price_feed_blind`, escalation after 30 minutes — instead of throwing out of the
alarm handler. **A feed that refuses to start is a visible outage; a feed
connected to the wrong venue is an invisible one**, and this build has now paid
for that distinction.

All existing `PriceFeed` tests configure `exchange: "gemini"` and passed
unchanged, which is itself the point: they were always testing the one venue the
hardcoded codec happened to be right for.

## PART 5 — THE TESTNET/PRODUCTION JUDGMENT CALL

`KrakenPriceFeedCodec.socketUrl` returns **the same real URL,
`wss://ws.kraken.com/v2`, for both `testnet` and `production`**, and throws on
any other `ENVIRONMENT`. This is a judgment call and is flagged as one.

**It does not reopen entry [90](90.md) DECISION 1.** That refusal —
`resolveKrakenExchange` declining to build a client in testnet — is an argument
about **ORDERS**: there is no Kraken sandbox, so a testnet client pointed at
`api.kraken.com` would trade real funds from the test environment. That argument
is entirely correct and is untouched.

It is not an argument about reading a public market, and the distinction was
verified rather than asserted (Part 6 of the session's trace):

* the codec's **entire runtime dependency graph is `shared/money.ts`** — no
  client, no signer, no credential module, not even imported;
* `socketUrl(environment: string | undefined)` takes a **plain string**, not the
  `Env` binding object, so credentials cannot reach it by signature;
* the outbound connection sends **exactly one header**,
  `{ Upgrade: "websocket" }` — no `Authorization`, no key, no nonce;
* the codec **cannot sign or place an order by construction**.

This follows the precedent **already established in this build** by
`exchange/kraken/public.ts` for the entry 97 cross-check client, which
deliberately has no environment parameter on its base URL, for the reason stated
there: *a reference price that is not the real market is not a reference.* Entry
[86](86.md)'s finding is that a sandbox publishes fiction with a fresh
timestamp. **Reading Kraken's real prices in testnet is more honest than
fabricating a number**, and pointing a testnet feed at a simulator would rebuild
inside the feed the exact fault this entry exists to remove from it.

In practice the path is unreachable: `venue-wiring.ts` refuses to create a
Kraken bot in testnet and `resolveKrakenExchange` refuses to build its client,
so no `kraken:*` feed can exist there. This is belt-and-braces.

**One honest caveat.** `Env` is a `declare global` augmentation, so
`this.env.KRAKEN_API_SECRET` would *compile* inside the `PriceFeed` Durable
Object. The isolation is enforced by the code not referencing it — verified by
search: zero occurrences of any secret, signer, client or credential symbol in
`price-feed.ts` — and **not** by a binding-level capability restriction.
Narrowing the DO's env type would make it structural. That is not done here and
is listed in Part 7.

## PART 6 — VERIFICATION

**`tsc --noEmit`: exit 0.**

**Full suite: 151 files, 4321 tests, all passing.** 57 of those are new
(49 codec + dispatch, 8 Durable Object routing).

Codec tests run against **frames captured verbatim from the live socket** —
`status`, a successful ack, a rejected ack, the bare heartbeat, the 9-row
snapshot and a single-row update — not hand-written to match the implementation.
The symbol table is the 12 spellings confirmed accepted live.

### The mutation test, which is the load-bearing evidence

A suite that passes proves nothing about a bug whose defining property was that
**everything passed while it was live**. So the bug was deliberately
reintroduced — `#codec()` changed back to ignore `config.exchange` and always
return Gemini's codec — and the suite re-run:

**6 of 8 routing tests failed**, reproducing the original signature exactly,
including:

```
- Expected                                          (binance: no socket at all)
+ Received
+ [ "wss://api.sandbox.gemini.com/v2/marketdata" ]
```

A venue with no feed codec silently dialling Gemini — **the precise failure this
entry documents**, caught by the new tests. The change was then reverted and
`tsc` plus the routing suite re-verified clean.

These are genuine regression tests, not tests that merely agree with the code
currently in front of them.

**Built and tested. NOT deployed. NOT restarted on any bot.** Both omissions are
deliberate.

**All three production bots remain halted** — `bot-hl8z62` (17:30:24Z),
`bot-vnjo24` (17:30:35Z), `bot-qfhkqo` (17:30:53Z) — with their real halt

feed is repointed, and should not be treated as a signal in the meantime.

### Next steps, in order

1. **Review and deploy this fix.** It is on disk only; production still runs the
   hardcoded-Gemini code.
2. **Verify a real Kraken price actually arrives.** A deploy is not proof. The
   feed must be observed delivering a price that matches Kraken's public ticker
   and *not* Gemini's — the two are currently ~0.07% and ~1.07% apart, which is
   enough to tell them apart deliberately even though it was never enough to
   trip the 2% cross-check.
3. **Then, separately and deliberately, decide about each halted bot.** Resuming
   is its own decision per bot, made after step 2 succeeds — not a consequence of
   deploying.

### Open items this session found and did not fix

* **The five-hour timestamp display discrepancy** (Part 1). Alert times were
  reported five hours behind their D1 `created_at`, labelled UTC. Unfixed,
  uninvestigated, and it actively misleads during an incident — it cost a wrong

* **`PriceFeed`'s env type is not narrowed** (Part 5). Credential isolation on
