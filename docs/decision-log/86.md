## PART 1 — WHAT TRIGGERED THIS, AND THE THREE THEORIES THAT WERE WRONG

`bot-ts1`, the first live trailing-stop bot, placed entry orders that never
filled. Fifteen of them across two runs. Each theory below was formed from real
evidence and killed by more of it; the order matters, because each wrong answer
was the reason the next question got asked.

**Theory 1 — a Gemini account-level heartbeat was cancelling our orders.**
Plausible: Gemini's "requires heartbeat" setting cancels every open order on a
key when the heartbeat lapses, and all 31 bots share one `gemini-main` account.

*Killed by:* querying every other bot's orders across the three cancellation
windows. **15 orders belonging to 15 other bots were open through all three
windows and none was cancelled** — some had been resting 6.9 to 13.3 days.
Across the whole `orders` table's history, the 55–65s cancellation band contains
**only** `bot-ts1`. An account-wide control would not have been so selective.

**Theory 2 — a fixed ~60-second venue-side timer.**
The lifetimes were extraordinarily consistent: 60.251s, 60.264s, 60.281s …
fourteen of them inside ±0.13s.

*Killed by:* reading where that number comes from. `bot-ts1` polls at
`POLL_INTERVAL_ROUTINE_MS = 60_000`, and Gemini sends no last-update time, so
`#foldTerminalState` stamps `orders.updated_at` with `remote.updatedAt ??
this.#now()` — **receipt time**. The "60 seconds" was our own poll cadence,
phase-locked to placement. It was never a measurement of the venue. Order 11's
48.268s outlier confirmed it: no fixed venue timer produces that, and it turned
out to be the manual halt's own cancel sweep.

**Theory 3 — the price feed was frozen, with a parsing bug behind it.**
Now genuinely close. Twelve orders across fifteen minutes were priced at exactly
`78172.34`, and the second run's crossing entries derived from that same number
43 minutes later. The feed's value was provably stuck.

*Killed by:* it is not a parsing bug. See PART 5 — the parser is correct, and
was verified against live captures from the venue itself.

**A fourth wrong turn worth recording, because it is the one that cost the most
time: the comparisons were against the wrong venue.** Both the investigation and
the operator benchmarked `78172.34` against `api.gemini.com` — production —
while `bot-ts1` trades on `api.sandbox.gemini.com`. Every "1.9% gap" in the first
three rounds was production-versus-sandbox divergence being read as feed
corruption.

## PART 2 — THE ACTUAL ROOT CAUSE

`ENVIRONMENT: "testnet"` resolves to Gemini's **sandbox** for both REST
(`GEMINI_BASE_URLS.sandbox`) and WebSocket (`GEMINI_WS_URLS.sandbox`). That is
correct and intended. **The sandbox's BTCUSD market is dead.**

| | sandbox | production |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bid | **68,169.88** | 76,865.53 |
| ask | **64,886.32** | 76,865.54 |
| last | **78,172.34** | 76,831.61 |
| 24h volume (BTC) | **0** | 213.96 |

Two impossibilities there. The book is **crossed** — the bid sits **$3,283 above
the ask**, which cannot happen on a venue that matches orders. And the sandbox's
1-minute candles show **1439 of the last 1440 identical** (`O=H=L=C=78172.34`),
every one with **zero volume**; exactly **two** distinct close values in a full
day.

**Our own orders were propping up the fake bid.** That 68,169.88 is, to the cent,
the resting buy price of nine of this account's own DCA bots. With no other
participants, the bots *were* the book.

This is not specific to BTCUSD: **199 of 360 sandbox symbols diverge more than 2%
from production** (ETHUSD −20.25%, DOGEUSD +15.63%, ALIUSD by a factor of
17,000).

**Neither trailing-stop fix built earlier tonight should be reverted.** Both are
correct and both stay:

- **The crossing-price entry (22.10) fixed a real design flaw.** The single
  trailing-stop entry inherited `#placeBuy`'s DCA maker pricing — a limit resting
  *at* the last trade price, which is right for a ladder that is meant to wait
  and wrong for a one-shot entry that must fill before the strategy can begin.
  That flaw was real in production terms and independent of the sandbox.
- **The retry cap did precisely its job.** It stopped the bot after three
  attempts instead of retrying forever into a dead market. It was designed
  against a symptom whose cause we had wrong, and it was still the right
  mechanism — arguably more valuable than intended, since "the venue's market is
  fake" is exactly the unbounded-retry condition no strategy can reason its way
  out of.

What the sandbox discovery changes is the **diagnosis**, not the code.

## PART 3 — THE STRUCTURAL GAP

`#forwardClosed` builds every forwarded price as:

```ts
const price: Price = { pair: candle.pair, price: candle.close, at };  // at = RECEIPT time
```

`at` is when this system **heard** the candle, not the candle's own timestamp.
That is deliberate, correct per section 5.6 ("when we heard"), and documented on
`Price`. **The consequence was never noticed: `lastPriceAt` is fresh by
construction on every delivery, no matter how old the value is.**

So both existing detectors were fully satisfied while the price was eleven hours
stale:

| Detector | Watches | Why it could not see this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `price_feed_blind` | socket heartbeats | the socket was healthy and heartbeating |
| `price_updates_stale` | `BotRuntimeState.lastPriceAt` | that field is stamped from receipt time, so it cannot go stale while deliveries continue |

**Both are liveness checks. Neither is a correctness check.** This is not an
oversight in either one — correctness had **no detector at all**, anywhere in the
system. The gap is system-wide: it is a property of how prices are stamped, not
of the trailing stop, not of Gemini, and not of this incident.

Section 5.6 governs data that **fails to arrive**. Nothing governed data that
**arrives and is wrong**. A well-formed candle carrying a fictional price
satisfies 5.6 completely.

## PART 4 — THE FIX: SPEC 5.7 AND THREE DETECTORS

New spec section **5.7, "Price-feed integrity: freshness is not correctness"**,
placed after 5.6 (which was section 5's last subsection, so nothing renumbered).

**(a) Frozen-value run detector** — `#trackFrozenValue`, called from
`#forwardClosed`, the single point every forwarded candle passes through. Counts
consecutive candles whose close is **unchanged AND** whose volume is **zero**;
raises a standing `price_feed_value_frozen` at **10 in a row**, naming the
market, the stuck value and the run length; resolves on the first differing
close.

*Why the conjunction.* Either signal alone is noisy — a thin pair has isolated
zero-volume minutes, a busy one repeats a close to the cent occasionally. A
market that does **both** for ten consecutive minutes is not a market. Ten
minutes on a 1-minute feed, against an incident that ran 1439 such candles: three
orders of magnitude of headroom, and it still fires within ten minutes of onset.

*Why it observes and never gates.* The detector runs **after** the forward, never
before. Withholding prices from a bot holding a position would trade a reporting
problem for a real risk one — a stop-loss cannot be evaluated against a price
that was suppressed for its own good. It says so; what to do is a human's call.
Every D1 write in it is wrapped, because a detector that can throw is a detector
that can stop a feed delivering.

*Standing, not repeating.* One row per incident (`frozenAlerted` latches it,
exactly as `escalated` latches the blind escalation). A row per frozen candle
would have been 1439 rows in a day, which is how an alert surface stops being
read.

**(b) Crossed-book refusal** — in `parsePrice`. `bid >= ask` is arithmetically
impossible on a venue that matches orders, so this needs **no threshold, no
tolerance and no tuning**; unlike every other plausibility check in the system it
has no false-positive case to trade against. `>=` rather than `>`, because a
locked book is not a real quote either.

Refusal is a `ParseError`, which the client already turns into `#unreadable`: a
definite, non-retryable failure that **5.6 already forbids from feeding strategy
logic**, and which every existing caller already handles. No new plumbing, no new
outcome kind for call sites to learn.

*This is the only place in the system a book is read.* Binance's
`/api/v3/ticker/price` returns a symbol and a price and no book at all, so there
is no counterpart to write on that side. Stated in the docblock so the asymmetry
does not later read as an omission.

**(c) Symbol validation** — `parseMessage` attributed every candle to the `pair`
its **caller** passed and never read the `symbol` the venue sends on every frame.
**Not the cause here**, and found along the way: each `PriceFeed` Durable Object
is named `exchange:pair` and subscribes its own socket to one symbol, so the
attribution is right by construction today. The v2 marketdata endpoint exists to
multiplex, though, and the day anyone uses it that way an unchecked attribution
is not a crash — it is every bot on the pair trading another market's candles,
well-formed and freshly stamped. Which is the exact class of failure 5.7 exists
for. Compared case-insensitively (REST lower-cases, the socket upper-cases); an
absent symbol is accepted rather than inventing a required field.

**(d) The operator warning**, which is not a detector. `GET /api/bots/:id` and
the creation response now carry a top-level `warning` string on testnet only.
See PART 6 for why it is separate from the three above.

## PART 5 — WHAT WAS VERIFIED, NOT ASSUMED

**The parser was verified against the venue, not against documentation.** Gemini's
docs pages are JS-rendered SPAs that return no readable content to a fetcher, so
both sockets were probed directly, read-only, with the exact frame
`GeminiPriceFeedCodec.subscribeMessage` sends:

```
SANDBOX     keys: changes,symbol,type   symbol:"BTCUSD"   1440 rows   NEWEST-FIRST
            newest [1788314220000, 76060.22, 76118.17, 76060.22, 76118.17, 0]
PRODUCTION  keys: changes,symbol,type   symbol:"BTCUSD"   1440 rows   NEWEST-FIRST
            newest [1788314220000, 76880.54, 76890.81, 76858.34, 76890.81, 0.0042272]
```

Identical shape on both. Row format `[timeMs, open, high, low, close, volume]`
matches what the codec assumes; `row[4]` is the close and the codec reads
`row[4]`; the wire is newest-first and `parseCandles` sorts ascending before the
Durable Object sees it. **The parser is correct and would work unchanged in
production. The bug was the venue's data, not the code reading it.**

**Negative control.** All three detectors were disabled and the suite re-run:
**10 tests failed**. Restored, all pass. None of the new tests is vacuous.

**One bug caught mid-implementation, and it would have been silent.** `#state` was
assigned from storage **wholesale** (`if (stored !== undefined) this.#state =
stored`). Every feed running when this ships has a blob written before 5.7's three
fields existed, so they would have loaded as `undefined`, `frozenRun + 1` would
have evaluated to `NaN`, and the detector would have **never fired on exactly the
longest-running feeds** — the ones most likely to be stuck. Fixed by spreading
over `INITIAL_STATE`, with a dedicated test that writes a pre-5.7 blob and
confirms the run still counts to ten.

**The test that matters most reproduces the real payload**, not an approximation:
`O=H=L=C=78172.34`, volume 0, ten consecutive, asserting the alert fires once as
`critical` naming market, value and run length. Its companions assert the two
false-positive cases the conjunction exists to exclude — an unchanged close that
actually traded (using the step 14 probe's own captured volumes), and zero-volume
candles whose price is moving.

*A test-writing note.* The first version of the run-reset test was wrong, and the
code was right: the engine's CURRENT-ONLY model forwards the **previous** candle,
so the message introducing a new price still finalises the old one and the run
ends one message after the market moves. The corrected test documents the lag
explicitly rather than working around it.

## PART 6 — WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER

**Cross-checking the feed against the same venue's REST endpoint would NOT have
caught this.** The sandbox's REST ticker reported the identical frozen
`78172.34`. Both sources agreed because **both were the same fiction**. That
check detects parser faults and stuck sockets; it cannot detect venue-level
staleness, and building it while believing otherwise would have closed this gap
on paper only.

The real fix for that is a **genuinely independent reference** — a second venue,
or a published index price. **Not built tonight**, recorded as future work, and
recorded in 5.7 as a known limit rather than pretended away.

**The three detectors are all after-the-fact.** They catch a freeze roughly ten
minutes in. They cannot tell an operator *before* they start a live test that the
venue they are about to trust is a simulator — which is the failure that actually
happened, and which cost four investigation rounds.

That is why the **static sandbox warning** exists as a separate thing:
environment-gated rather than condition-gated, because "the testnet venue is a
simulator" is a standing property of the deployment, true whether or not any
particular feed is stuck. Absent rather than `null` off testnet, so a production
payload stays byte-identical to what it was before the field existed — the same
treatment `proposalLink` gets.

It is on `GET /api/bots/:id` and the creation response. **Deliberately not on
`GET /api/price-feeds`**, which returns a bare array: attaching a top-level field
there would change the response from array to object, which is a breaking shape
change and not the small addition this was meant to be.
